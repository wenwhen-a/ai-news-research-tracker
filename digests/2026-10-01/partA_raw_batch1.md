## Physis-Lang: Self-Evolving Language as a Physical Representation for Video World Model
- **arXiv:** 2609.40358 · https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.40358
- **Submitted:** 2026-09-30
- **Authors:** Liming Lu et al. (17 authors)
- **Qualifying affiliation(s):** NVIDIA — Liming Lu, Wenhang Ge, Yuchao Gu, Yunze Liu, Han Cai, Ming-Yu Liu, Song Han et al. (most of the author list); co-authors also from University of Oxford and MIT
- **Categories:** cs.CV
- **Open release:** none (no code, weights, or demo link found on the abstract page or in the paper)
- **Shipped counterpart:** none found

**Summary (≤3 sentences):** Video world models often generate visually plausible videos that violate basic physical principles, and this paper argues language has not yet been fully exploited as a representation of physical processes. Physis-Lang is a self-evolving agentic framework that constructs, evaluates and refines structured language descriptions of physical causes, interactions, principles, and effects, then uses them to curate training data and fine-tune video diffusion backbones. A companion benchmark, PhysCapBench (246 videos, 3,794 assertions), scores captions by precision/recall of physical claims to drive iterative refinement.
**Purpose (≤3 sentences):** The authors aim to make physical plausibility an explicit, optimizable training signal rather than something video generators must infer implicitly from pixels. Language is used both to diagnose a model's specific physical failure modes and to retrieve/curate training videos targeting those weaknesses.
**Breakthrough (≤3 sentences):** The authors report consistent improvements on four physical-video benchmarks when fine-tuning Wan and Cosmos3 backbones with Physis-Lang-curated data: PhyGenBench 61.67→71.04, Physics-IQ Verified 40.23→43.41, VideoPhy-2 60.41→68.02, and PhyGround 65.18→69.90. They report that their Cosmos3-Nano-based models surpass the proprietary Veo 3.1 model on three of these four benchmarks, and that distilling a 4B "PhysThinker" vision-language model cut their re-captioning cost from about $24.12K to near $0.
**Tools & method (≤3 sentences):** The pipeline combines an iterative, GPT-5.5-based physics-aware captioning/critic loop, deficiency-guided video retrieval that converts model weaknesses into text queries, and fine-tuning on a curated 183K video-caption dataset (71K filtered WISA-80K + 112K retrieved). Training used 16 GPUs across two nodes with bfloat16 precision and fully sharded data parallelism.
**Limitation (≤3 sentences):** The paper has no explicit limitations section; by its own description, the initial captioning/critic stage depends on access to GPT-5.5, and reported gains are validated only on the four physical-video benchmarks tested (PhyGenBench, Physics-IQ Verified, VideoPhy-2, PhyGround).

## VR-JEPA: Learning Contrastive-State Latent Guidance for Generation-based Video Reasoning
- **arXiv:** 2609.40129 · https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.40129
- **Submitted:** 2026-09-30
- **Authors:** Zehua Ma et al. (12 authors)
- **Qualifying affiliation(s):** Tencent (author affiliation #4 in the paper's institution list)
- **Categories:** cs.CV
- **Open release:** none (no code, weights, or demo link found)
- **Shipped counterpart:** none found

**Summary (≤3 sentences):** The paper targets generation-based visual reasoning, where a video diffusion model is used to "think" by generating a rollout, but plain generation lacks guidance on which visual states matter for the reasoning task and is prone to physical/structural inconsistencies. VR-JEPA predicts task-relevant latent trajectories with a frozen V-JEPA encoder and a learnable predictor, then uses those latents to guide video generation. It is evaluated on VBVR-Pro-Bench, a 500-instance visual reasoning benchmark spanning five task categories.
**Purpose (≤3 sentences):** The authors want generation-based reasoning models to focus supervision on the visual states and regions that are actually informative for a task, rather than optimizing uniformly over every generated frame. They also want a single model to handle multiple distinct reasoning skills without degrading on any one of them.
**Breakthrough (≤3 sentences):** The authors report an 11.33% relative improvement over "the cutting-edge generation-based reasoning baseline" on VBVR-Pro-Bench, moving overall accuracy from 50.3% to 56.0%. In-domain accuracy rose from 55.4% to 65.9%, while out-of-domain accuracy rose more modestly from 45.1% to 46.1%.
**Tools & method (≤3 sentences):** The method has two parts: Localized Contrastive-State Learning (LCL), which contrasts successful videos against task-matched generated candidates in V-JEPA feature space to weight supervision toward informative states/regions, and a Skill-Routed Mixture of Experts (SR-MoE) with seven skill-specific experts (e.g., spatial reasoning, numerical reasoning, dynamics) combined via adaptive routing. Training used eight H100 GPUs (64GB each) and compared against six open-source video models (Wan2.1/2.2 variants) plus proprietary video models.
**Limitation (≤3 sentences):** The authors' own ablations show the gain is much smaller out-of-domain (1.0 point) than in-domain (10.5 points), indicating the contrastive-state guidance generalizes less robustly to held-out reasoning types. The paper also notes that publicly released VBVR-Pro-Bench baseline outputs differ in frame count/resolution, requiring the authors to rerun baselines for fair comparison.

## Reconstructing the Dynamic World: A Representation-Centric View of 4D Scene Reconstruction
- **arXiv:** 2609.39960 · https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39960
- **Submitted:** 2026-09-30
- **Authors:** Ziren Gong et al. (16 authors)
- **Qualifying affiliation(s):** Google DeepMind — Ming-Hsuan Yang ("is with Google DeepMind and the University of California, Merced"); FLAG: borderline not applied (Google/DeepMind is on the explicit qualifying list), though this is a survey/review paper rather than a new-method paper
- **Categories:** cs.CV
- **Open release:** none for a trained model (this is a survey); the authors maintain a companion reading-list repo at github.com/ZiyangYan/Awesome-4D-Scene-Reconstruction, described as "an evolving collection of relevant papers and resources" rather than code or weights
- **Shipped counterpart:** none found

**Summary (≤3 sentences):** This is a survey/unifying-framework paper on 4D (dynamic) scene reconstruction, covering both implicit Neural Radiance Field (4D NeRF) and explicit 3D Gaussian Splatting (4DGS) approaches. It organizes existing methods by scene representation, temporal modeling strategy, reconstruction pipeline, and optimization objective, and consolidates 22 datasets and evaluation metrics used across the field. The authors state that despite progress, "reconstructing dynamic scenes remains challenging due to non-rigid motion, occlusions, temporal inconsistencies, and trade-offs between reconstruction fidelity and computational efficiency."
**Purpose (≤3 sentences):** The paper aims to give the field a structured, representation-centric taxonomy connecting design choices (how a scene and its motion are represented) to measured outcomes (geometric fidelity, appearance consistency, efficiency), since prior methods' relationships were "fragmented." It is intended as a reference for understanding existing approaches and identifying open research directions rather than proposing a new method.
**Breakthrough (≤3 sentences):** As a survey, it does not claim a new result of its own; it compiles comparative numbers from the literature, e.g. on the Neu3D benchmark FreeTimeGS reports PSNR 33.19/LPIPS 0.036 and TaylorGaussian reports PSNR 33.02/SSIM 0.970, while on NuScenes geometric reconstruction STGC-NeRF reports Chamfer Distance 0.22/F-Score 0.91. Efficiency comparisons on Neu3D span from 4DRotorGS (277 FPS, 1.0 hour training) to StreamRF (10.9 FPS, 0.3 hour training), illustrating the quality/speed trade-off the paper discusses.
**Tools & method (≤3 sentences):** The survey's taxonomy splits methods into four 4D-NeRF categories (deformation-field, implicit 4D primitive, 4D feature-volume, temporal-prior-based) and three 4DGS categories (explicit 4D primitive, deformation-field, frame-wise training). It consolidates 22 datasets across synthetic, monocular/sparse real-world, dense multi-view, and long-horizon/multimodal driving settings (e.g. D-NeRF, HyperNeRF, Neu3DV, Waymo, nuScenes), with efficiency figures measured on four NVIDIA A100 GPUs.
**Limitation (≤3 sentences):** The authors state that long-range spatio-temporal coherence over extended sequences remains an open problem, and that the field lacks "standardized protocols for evaluating temporal coherence, topology changes, and long-term consistency." They also note feed-forward models face a "fidelity ceiling in out-of-distribution scenarios" due to reliance on pretrained dataset biases, and that explicit 4D primitives carry substantial memory overhead from higher per-primitive parameter dimensionality.

## DyRAD: Radar Novel View Synthesis for Dynamic Driving Scenes
- **arXiv:** 2609.39841 · https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39841
- **Submitted:** 2026-09-30
- **Authors:** Merav Keidar, Tomer Borreda, Rajalakshmi Nandakumar, Or Litany
- **Qualifying affiliation(s):** NVIDIA — Or Litany
- **Categories:** cs.CV
- **Open release:** code — github.com/Dyrad-NVS/DyRad (includes a deterministic generator for the paper's synthetic dataset); project page at dyrad-nvs.github.io
- **Shipped counterpart:** none found

**Summary (≤3 sentences):** DyRAD addresses radar-based novel view synthesis for dynamic autonomous-driving scenes, where prior methods struggle to render Doppler signatures for moving objects, separate true scene geometry from sensor-induced measurement spreading, and evaluate synthesis at viewpoints away from the recorded trajectory. It represents scenes as static background reflectors plus motion-tracked dynamic point reflectors, rendered through a differentiable, physics-grounded renderer with a fixed analytic point-spread function (PSF) per sensor. This lets reconstructed scenes be re-rendered under different radar sensor configurations without retraining ("zero-shot sensor-configuration transfer").
**Purpose (≤3 sentences):** The goal is to let researchers evaluate autonomous-driving perception stacks at radar viewpoints that were never actually recorded, by synthesizing physically faithful range-azimuth-Doppler (RAD) tensors for dynamic scenes. Separating the fixed sensor PSF from scene geometry is meant to prevent sensor artifacts from being baked into the learned scene representation.
**Breakthrough (≤3 sentences):** The authors report off-path detection recovery of 91.7% of reference-detected objects versus 18.1% for the strongest baseline, and on-path full-RAD correlation improving from 0.068 to 0.272 with foreground hit rate rising from 26.9% to 90.7% (RADIal dataset). In ablations, adding Doppler supervision reportedly cut vehicle Doppler error by 60%, and using the fixed analytic PSF doubled detection recall versus a learned/unconstrained alternative.
**Tools & method (≤3 sentences):** The method couples zero-extent point reflectors (background + tracked dynamic objects) with a fixed, physics-grounded per-sensor PSF kernel, projecting reflector velocities onto the line-of-sight to render RAD tensors with Doppler used as both output and supervision signal. It is evaluated on RADIal (ten 60-frame sequences, full RAD), Boreas (three 50-frame sequences, RA-only), and a five-scene synthetic benchmark with ground-truth off-path poses, all run on a single NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4090.
**Limitation (≤3 sentences):** The authors state that reflector-to-object assignments rely on object annotations and remain fixed during optimization, and that the method uses a ground-plane implementation because elevation is absent from the measurements. They also note that modeling multipath and speckle noise could improve measurement realism, and evaluation is limited to processed radar configurations rather than raw physical sensor data.

## Lens Flare Removal and Reconstruction
- **arXiv:** 2609.39527 · https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39527
- **Submitted:** 2026-09-30
- **Authors:** Tarun Yenamandra, Jonathon Luiten, Daniel Cremers, Nathan Matsuda
- **Qualifying affiliation(s):** Meta — Jonathon Luiten (Meta Reality Labs, New York) and Nathan Matsuda (Meta Reality Labs Research, Redmond); Tarun Yenamandra's work was done during a Meta Reality Labs Research internship, with primary affiliation TU Munich (MCML)
- **Categories:** cs.CV; cs.GR
- **Open release:** demo — project page at https://lensflare-3dgs.pages.dev (per the arXiv Comments field: "20 pages, 14 figures. Project page: [this https URL]"); no GitHub code link found
- **Shipped counterpart:** none found

**Summary (≤3 sentences):** Lens flares are camera artifacts that degrade downstream applications such as 3D scene reconstruction, and existing removal methods handle small flares but struggle with large, full-frame reflective flares; consistent flare representation across multiple viewpoints had not previously been explored. This paper fine-tunes a diffusion model (Difix3D+ with LoRA adapters) on a new large-reflective-flare dataset for removal, and separately introduces an explicit, re-renderable 3D flare representation anchored to a camera-near plane that is jointly optimized with Gaussian splatting to decompose scenes into flare and non-flare components.
**Purpose (≤3 sentences):** The authors want to both remove large reflective lens flares from individual images and model flares explicitly in 3D so they can be edited, removed, or transferred consistently across views and scenes during multi-view/Gaussian-splatting reconstruction. This addresses a gap where prior flare work treated flares as 2D image artifacts rather than view-consistent 3D phenomena.
**Breakthrough (≤3 sentences):** The authors report PSNR of 26.68 dB on the Flare7K++ benchmark and 27.06 dB on their new VFX benchmark, stated to outperform prior methods, while their flare removal model trains in about 20 hours versus more than 4 days for baselines. Their 3D flare+scene Gaussian-splatting representation renders in real time at 92.7 FPS at 1920×1080 on an RTX 4090.
**Tools & method (≤3 sentences):** The removal model is a Difix3D+ diffusion model fine-tuned with LoRA on a combined dataset of real large reflective flares (1,866 images from 90 ActionVFX visual-effects clips) and procedurally generated flares, plus the clean Flickr24K dataset. The 3D flare model represents flares as Gaussians constrained to lie along the line between the camera's principal point and the projected light source, jointly optimized with scene Gaussian splatting across 99 captured 3D scenes (and evaluated on 55 scenes for reconstruction quality).
**Limitation (≤3 sentences):** The paper does not include an explicit "Limitations" section, but it notes the method requires approximate 3D light-source positions, whose automatic detection "can be noisy," and that per-pixel ground-truth flare/scene separation is intractable to obtain from real captures, so evaluation relies on model-derived references rather than true ground truth for flare reconstruction. The flare representation also assumes the light source stays visible across the capture sequence.

## PartiCam: Camera Controlled Video Generation with Reward Guidance
- **arXiv:** 2609.39504 · https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39504
- **Submitted:** 2026-09-30
- **Authors:** Amine Ouasfi, Runjia Li, Junlin Han, Eric Marchand, Philip H.S. Torr, Adnane Boukhayma
- **Qualifying affiliation(s):** Meta — Junlin Han (affiliation listed as Meta and University of Oxford)
- **Categories:** cs.CV; cs.AI
- **Open release:** none (no code, weights, or demo link found; the lead's claim of a Tencent affiliation could not be verified — no Tencent mention appears anywhere in the paper)
- **Shipped counterpart:** none found

**Summary (≤3 sentences):** PartiCam is a training-free, inference-time method for making video diffusion models follow a specified camera trajectory, addressing drift in existing score-modulation guidance and the data/generalization cost of training-based camera-control approaches. It combines a global Sequential Monte-Carlo (SMC) stage that propagates and reweights a population of candidate video trajectories by reward score, with a local particle-filtering restart (PF-Restart) stage that perturbs and resamples candidates at selected denoising timesteps.
**Purpose (≤3 sentences):** The authors want precise, controllable camera trajectories in generated video without retraining the underlying video diffusion model, and without the instability/quality degradation of naive restart-based sampling schemes. They frame accurate, 3D-grounded camera control as "a key step toward the vision of comprehensive world models."
**Breakthrough (≤3 sentences):** On static scenes, the authors report an Absolute Trajectory Error (ATE) of 0.526 versus 0.767 for the NVS-Solver baseline; on dynamic scenes, ATE improves from a baseline 2.308 to 0.807. They summarize this as roughly a 6× reduction in translation error and 4× reduction in ATE relative to NVS-Solver.
**Tools & method (≤3 sentences):** The method layers global SMC trajectory exploration (propagation, reward-based weight update, resampling) with local guided restarts, run over roughly 100 diffusion iterations with local refinement active at iterations 8–32 and global SMC at 8–40, both updated every 4 iterations. It is evaluated on six Tanks and Temples scenes plus three additional static scenes, and nine monocular dynamic urban/natural videos, using Particle-SFM-estimated trajectories, RPE/ATE, and FID.
**Limitation (≤3 sentences):** The authors state the method can require a larger denoising budget because it maintains multiple candidate trajectories simultaneously, increasing inference cost. They also note effectiveness depends on reward function design, which "may require task-specific tuning" for different scenes or control objectives.
