## WinDEX: Metric 3D Hand Motion Reconstruction in the Wild for Dexterous Teleoperation and Policy Learning
- **arXiv:** 2611.21143 · https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21143
- **Submitted:** 2026-11-16 (v1)
- **Authors:** Hongyu Ma, Hairong Qu, Shiqi Zhao, Yongsong Yang, Kai Shi, Jiahao Ai, Xin You, Peng Yin
- **Qualifying affiliation(s):** FLAG: borderline — CyberOrigin (Xin You); other co-authors are at City University of Hong Kong
- **Categories:** cs.RO
- **Open release:** demo — project website https://fruitrabbit.github.io/WinDEX/ (no code, weights, or public dataset release mentioned in the visible text)
- **Shipped counterpart:** none found

**Summary (≤3 sentences):** Collecting large-scale, metrically accurate 3D hand-motion data in the wild is hard, limiting data for training dexterous robot hands. WinDEX combines geometric-temporal optimization with visual-inertial SLAM to extract metric-scale 3D hand motion from in-the-wild egocentric stereo video, an end-to-end fisheye-native reconstruction network (WinDEX-Net), distilled streaming/offline models, and a low-cost stereo-inertial capture rig (CyberCap), producing the 1,000-hour WinDEX-Hand dataset. The authors report state-of-the-art reconstruction accuracy and large task-success-rate gains for policies trained on this data versus prior motion references.

**Purpose (≤3 sentences):** To produce accurate, metric-scale, real-time-capable 3D hand motion reconstruction from cheap in-the-wild video, for use in training dexterous robot manipulation policies and for live teleoperation.

**Breakthrough (≤3 sentences):** The authors report offline reconstruction running at 43.61 FPS and causal streaming at 171 FPS, with state-of-the-art held-out accuracy of 14.5 pixels in-the-wild reprojection error and 37.29 mm world-frame joint error (with visual-inertial odometry). They report 7.6x and 3.6x the task success rates of reinforcement-learning policies trained with WiLoR and HaWoR motion references, respectively, and demonstrate real-time arm-hand teleoperation on a physical 20-joint "Wuji" robot hand.

**Tools & method (≤3 sentences):** The pipeline integrates geometric-temporal optimization for metric 3D hand annotation with visual-inertial SLAM for world-frame grounding, a fisheye-native stereo network (WinDEX-Net) trained via a model-assisted "data-model flywheel," and distilled models for offline vs. causal streaming inference; training used 4x RTX 4090 GPUs, offline inference/speed tests used a single RTX 4090 (FP16), and the CyberCap capture rig uses 1920x1200 fisheye stereo at 30 fps plus a 300 Hz IMU.

**Limitation (≤3 sentences):** The authors state that during occlusion the method holds the last high-confidence pose rather than recovering unobserved articulation, and that the causal real-time streaming model (WinDEX-Stream) is measurably less accurate than the offline windowed model (e.g., 42.7 mm vs. 20.3 mm wrist error at comparable settings). They also note their visual-inertial odometry camera-trajectory estimate carries 0.150 m RMS absolute trajectory error and 11.69° RMS orientation error against optical motion capture, which propagates into the reported world-frame hand error, and that their physical-robot experiments relied on external camera-based pose tracking as an approximation of simulation-level ground truth.

## UNITE: Consolidating Embodied VLM Specialists via Shared-Anchor One-Bit Factorization
- **arXiv:** 2611.20767 · https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20767
- **Submitted:** 2026-11-16 (v1)
- **Authors:** Tao Tao, Anke Tang, Enneng Yang, Zhi Hou, Xiaogang Wang, Dacheng Tao
- **Qualifying affiliation(s):** ACE Robotics, Shanghai — Tao Tao, Zhi Hou, Xiaogang Wang. FLAG: borderline — ACE Robotics (not on the explicit qualified-company list; standing unclear, kept per borderline rule). Other authors: Sichuan University, Nanyang Technological University, Sun Yat-sen University (Shenzhen).
- **Categories:** cs.LG (primary), cs.CV
- **Open release:** none (no GitHub/HuggingFace/project page found in abstract or HTML body)
- **Shipped counterpart:** none found

**Summary (≤3 sentences):** UNITE consolidates multiple fine-tuned vision-language-model (VLM) "specialists" — for visual grounding, spatial reasoning, navigation, and driving understanding — into a single compact mixture-of-experts model instead of deploying them separately or merging them into one conflict-prone dense model. It keeps each specialist's feed-forward block as a routable expert, compressed via a shared floating-point "anchor" plus per-expert binary deviations, and adapts only the router. Built from four Qwen3-VL-8B specialists, it is evaluated on 22 benchmarks plus R2R Val-Unseen navigation.

**Purpose (≤3 sentences):** The authors aim to avoid the storage cost of deploying many separate embodied VLM specialists while avoiding the capability loss and parameter conflicts that come from naively merging them into a single dense model. UNITE targets near-single-model storage while retaining each specialist's expertise.

**Breakthrough (≤3 sentences):** The authors report the full UNITE model occupies 20.3 GB — 1.16× the storage of one dense specialist and 59.6% below the uncompressed mixture-of-experts — while updating fewer than 0.6M router parameters (under 0.01% of the 8.8B active parameters), using only source weights with no calibration data. They report it matches six static merging methods in mean retention of best-specialist performance, leads all of them on Scan2Cap, ScanQA, and all four navigation metrics (up to +19.1 on Scan2Cap over the best source specialist), and is nearly lossless relative to the uncompressed MoE on 21 of 22 benchmarks.

**Tools & method (≤3 sentences):** UNITE represents the expert bank through a "shared-anchor one-bit factorization" — a shared floating-point anchor plus expert-specific, row-scaled binary deviations — optimized jointly via closed-form alternating updates with no calibration data, then adapts only token-level routing with the backbone and experts frozen.

**Limitation (≤3 sentences):** The authors report one clear exception to otherwise near-lossless compression: on VSI-Bench, frame-count experiments suggest the factorized model has impaired use of cross-frame evidence compared to the uncompressed mixture-of-experts.

## Reference Forcing: Long Video Generation with Region-Guided Drift Correction
- **arXiv:** 2611.21559 · https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21559
- **Submitted:** 2026-11-16 (v1)
- **Authors:** Sheng Zhao, Haitian Zheng, Weikai Lin, Junjie Zhao, Yuhao Zhu
- **Qualifying affiliation(s):** Adobe — Haitian Zheng (co-authors otherwise at University of Rochester)
- **Categories:** cs.CV
- **Open release:** code/demo — project page with visualizations and code at https://zhaosheng-thu.github.io/reference-force/ (no model weights mentioned)
- **Shipped counterpart:** none found

**Summary (≤3 sentences):** Long autoregressive video generators drift in subject/background appearance over time because they are distilled over short windows and KV-cache history can itself have drifted. The authors propose Reference Forcing, which anchors generation to the very first frames as a ground-truth appearance reference instead of relying on potentially-drifted history. It reports state-of-the-art long-video subject/background consistency, with the advantage growing as videos get longer.

**Purpose (≤3 sentences):** To stop long (minutes-scale) autoregressively generated videos from losing a consistent subject/background appearance over time, a known failure of distillation-based fast video generators.

**Breakthrough (≤3 sentences):** The authors report that a teacher model forces each region of a new frame to attend to the matching region in the initial frames (found via off-the-shelf segmentation), then distills this into a student attention path needing no external models at inference, plus a "value correction" step that pulls drifted KV-cache values back toward the clean initial-frame values. On 128 MovieGenBench prompts at 120 seconds, they report 97.27 subject-consistency vs. 97.06 for the LongLive baseline, ranking first overall on VBench-Long at 30s and 60s, and winning 78% of pairwise VLM-judge comparisons against LongLive.

**Tools & method (≤3 sentences):** Built on top of frozen LongLive-1.3B and Self-Forcing 1.3B base models (only the new "anchor path" is trained), evaluated with VBench-Long metrics, DINOv2-based L-Consistency, MiraBench tracking strength, a 7-participant user study, and a VLM judge, against baselines MemRoPE, LongLive-RAG, ∞-RoPE, and Deep Forcing.

**Limitation (≤3 sentences):** The authors state that only the first chunk serves as a reference, so objects entering the scene later have no reference frame to anchor to, and updating the reference mid-generation is left to future work; they also note slightly lower imaging quality than LongLive (attributed to anchor-path averaging smoothing fine textures) and that the teacher is costly to run, and scenes with several similar subjects remain difficult since the teacher's segmentation tracks a single subject.

## MovieRoll: A Systematic Framework for Rolling Out Long-Form Videos
- **arXiv:** 2611.21339 · https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21339
- **Submitted:** 2026-11-16 (v1)
- **Authors:** Yunyang Ge, Weijie Kong, Ryan Xu, Yue Wu, Qi Tian, Jiangfeng Xiong, Bin Lin, Mingzhe Zheng, Shenghai Yuan, Zheyuan Liu, Jianwei Zhang, Li Yuan
- **Qualifying affiliation(s):** Tencent Hunyuan (one of three affiliations listed on the paper, alongside Peking University and HKUST; exact per-author mapping was not extractable from the HTML, but a footnote notes some authors' work was "done during an internship at Tencent Hunyuan," indicating Tencent Hunyuan staff co-authorship)
- **Categories:** cs.CV
- **Open release:** code + weights — project page https://Tencent-Hunyuan.github.io/MovieRoll/, GitHub https://github.com/Tencent-Hunyuan/MovieRoll, Hugging Face https://huggingface.co/yunyangge/MovieRoll
- **Shipped counterpart:** none found

**Summary (≤3 sentences):** Streaming video generators must sustain quality beyond their training horizon while managing growing self-generated history and KV-cache costs. MovieRoll is a base-to-distilled framework connecting streaming fine-tuning, long-rollout distillation, and long-horizon inference to address this. The authors report it remains competitive at 30s after training only on up to 15s clips, and that the distilled variant achieves the highest total scores among compared methods up to 60s.

**Purpose (≤3 sentences):** To let streaming/autoregressive video diffusion models generate stable, high-quality long-form video well beyond the duration they were trained on, without exploding cache memory or losing motion naturalness.

**Breakthrough (≤3 sentences):** The authors report their hierarchical KV-cache compression (HieraKV), which keeps full history in shallow layers and sink-plus-recent history in deeper layers, reduces cache memory by up to 78% while improving generation quality; their cumulative long-tuning distillation (CuLT-DMD) enables efficient long-rollout training of a few-step generator by averaging local gradients while holding parameters fixed during each rollout. On HeliosBench, MovieRoll-Distilled is reported as highest-scoring among compared methods on single- and multi-event evaluation up to 60s.

**Tools & method (≤3 sentences):** The framework combines Window-wise Teacher Forcing (bidirectional attention within windows, causal access to prior history) for a multi-step base model, HieraKV cache compression, CuLT-DMD distillation for a few-step generator, KV recomputation and guidance annealing (AnnealCFG) to extend beyond the training horizon, and FBCA attention plus a PS-VAE decoder for efficient streaming output; inference/efficiency measurements were run on NVIDIA H20 GPUs (up to 8 GPUs).

**Limitation (≤3 sentences):** The authors state MovieRoll-Distilled "has not yet achieved real-time generation" — even on 8 H20 GPUs with SageAttention, a 60s video generates at 9.47 FPS versus the 24 FPS needed for real-time playback — and that MovieRoll-Base is insufficiently stable at 60s (so only the distilled model was evaluated at that length), with semantic and drifting-naturalness ratings dropping at 60s versus 10s. They also note, based on their own experience rather than measurement, that streaming models still struggle with complex motion and camera trajectories.

## DuoFlow: Towards Synchronized Speech and Motion Generation with Fine-Grained Control
- **arXiv:** 2611.21530 · https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21530
- **Submitted:** 2026-11-16 (v1)
- **Authors:** Jun Dong, Ronghui Li, Zhongyuan Hu, Yunqi Zhao, Lijie Guo, Ruizhang Xu, Di Kang, Linchao Bao, Xiu Li
- **Qualifying affiliation(s):** Tencent — Di Kang, Linchao Bao (other co-authors at Tsinghua University)
- **Categories:** cs.CV
- **Open release:** none — only a project page (https://jundong-dev.github.io/DuoFlow-project-page/) is mentioned; no code or weights release stated
- **Shipped counterpart:** none found

**Summary (≤3 sentences):** Existing joint speech-motion generation models only implicitly condition on transcript semantics or speech features, giving little explicit control over motion hierarchy, body parts, or vocal style. The authors build MotionSpeech, a new benchmark with transcripts, voice-style descriptions, and two-level hierarchical motion conditions, and propose DuoFlow, a dual-stream latent flow transformer that jointly denoises speech and motion. DuoFlow is reported to substantially outperform cascade and joint-generation baselines on speech quality, motion-contact fidelity, and semantic alignment.

**Purpose (≤3 sentences):** To give embodied-speaker generation (synchronized voice + body motion) fine-grained, explicit control over vocal style and motion at the body-part/hierarchy level, rather than only implicit control via text or speech embeddings.

**Breakthrough (≤3 sentences):** The authors report DuoFlow achieves 4.71% word error rate and 0.94 speaker similarity versus 6.12%/0.80 for a CosyVoice2+MambaTalk cascade baseline, and 87.43%/90.40% pairing accuracy at two granularity levels versus the next-best baseline's 61.37%/62.68% (Motion-Omni). They also report DuoFlow is roughly 2–4x faster at inference (1.33s for a 12.9s clip, RTF 0.10) than the cascade and Motion-Omni baselines.

**Tools & method (≤3 sentences):** DuoFlow uses a dual-stream latent flow transformer with a shared noise level and a unified second-based temporal coordinate, modality-specific residual connectors to inject hierarchical conditions, layer-wise bidirectional cross-modal attention, and a monotonic phoneme pathway with CTC supervision for speech-motion temporal alignment; it was trained on the new MotionSpeech dataset (34,950 clips, ~125.2 hours) using 8×A100 GPUs per stage.

**Limitation (≤3 sentences):** The authors state in their conclusion that "improving physical contact fidelity and extending the framework to streaming generation remain future directions," and their own reported foot-skating rate (29.61%) is noticeably higher than the CosyVoice2+MambaTalk cascade baseline's 46.78%... (note: DuoFlow's 29.61% is actually lower/better than all compared baselines, but still a double-digit skating rate the authors implicitly flag via the contact-fidelity future-work note).

## Conflict-Aware Multi-Teacher On-Policy Distillation in Aligned Action Spaces for Cross-Game Generalization
- **arXiv:** 2611.21094 · https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21094
- **Submitted:** 2026-11-16 (v1)
- **Authors:** Jing Yi, Cehao Yang, Hualei Zhang, Haitao Li
- **Qualifying affiliation(s):** FLAG: borderline — LIGHTSPEED (Jing Yi, Haitao Li; likely Tencent's Lightspeed Studios, not independently confirmed beyond the paper's own "LIGHTSPEED" affiliation label); other co-authors at HKUST(GZ)/HKUST
- **Categories:** cs.AI
- **Open release:** none found (paper page links only to the arXiv abstract/PDF)
- **Shipped counterpart:** none found

**Summary (≤3 sentences):** The authors target zero-shot cross-game generalization: consolidating several single-game vision-language-model (VLM) experts into one model that can control unseen games from raw pixels with no target-game training. They identify two problems in scaling multi-teacher on-policy distillation to autoregressive VLMs — token-to-action misalignment and conflicting corrections from teachers trained under incompatible game dynamics — and propose CAMOPD to resolve both. On held-out games, CAMOPD is reported to outperform prompting, weight-merging, and multi-task baselines.

**Purpose (≤3 sentences):** To build a single VLM game-playing controller that generalizes zero-shot to new games it was never trained on, by better combining knowledge distilled from multiple single-game "teacher" experts without the teachers' conflicting advice canceling out.

**Breakthrough (≤3 sentences):** The authors report CAMOPD achieves 86.7% (26/30) and 80.0% (24/30) zero-shot Task-1 success on the unseen games GeoDash and Doodle Jump respectively, using a Qwen3.5-4B backbone with rank-16 LoRA fine-tuning, evaluated on GameWorld's "Paused 100-Step Reset-on-Fail" protocol (30 executions per task). Their method combines "complete-action alignment" (normalizing command-completion likelihoods over each game's legal action set) with cross-evaluating specialist teachers to upweight compatible ones and suppress conflicting corrections, without needing any target-game data.

**Tools & method (≤3 sentences):** CAMOPD is a post-training (distillation) framework built on a Qwen3.5-4B VLM with LoRA (rank 16, attention + MLP projections, sequence length 2048, AdamW), evaluated on the GameWorld benchmark (Ouyang et al., 2026) using temporal-control (GeoDash) and directional-steering (Doodle Jump) transfer suites against prompting, weight-merging, and multi-task distillation baselines.

**Limitation (≤3 sentences):** The authors note their evaluation protocol pauses the game during each VLM inference step over discrete commands, which they acknowledge differs from "real-time continuous streams." They also state the student model only sees a short 1-2 frame window with no explicit long-horizon episodic memory, and list streaming control and memory-augmented policies as future work.

## BlockAlign: Preference Optimization for Trajectory-Distilled Video Generation
- **arXiv:** 2611.20351 · https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20351
- **Submitted:** 2026-11-16 (v1)
- **Authors:** Jingyuan Zhu, Biaolong Chen, Yangyang Qin, Shengyang Shen, Yaohua Wang, Weiming Bai, Miao Lu, Aixi Zhang
- **Qualifying affiliation(s):** Alibaba Group — all eight authors (contact emails on alibaba-inc.com domain).
- **Categories:** cs.CV
- **Open release:** none (no GitHub/HuggingFace/project page found in abstract or HTML body)
- **Shipped counterpart:** none found

**Summary (≤3 sentences):** BlockAlign is a preference-optimization framework for aligning few-step, "trajectory-distilled" video generators (e.g., Parallel Decoding Distillation and Parametric Trajectory Distillation models) without teacher fine-tuning or re-distillation. It fixes a mismatch between standard flow-matching preference objectives, which assume single-head local velocity supervision, and the finite-interval, block-average transition interface these distilled generators actually use. The authors test it on multi-reference video generation using Alibaba's in-house RefVid-V4 model and the MiniMax-H3 Ref2VA model.

**Purpose (≤3 sentences):** The goal is to directly align deployed few-step video generators on their native sampling interface — cutting rollout costs relative to full-trajectory alignment — rather than naively supervising only the primary velocity sub-head, which the authors show induces a training-inference mismatch (their "DirectAlign" baseline) that degrades quality below the unaligned model.

**Breakthrough (≤3 sentences):** The authors report BlockAlign unlocks the underlying distilled model's (PDD) 5x few-step rollout speedup, yielding 3x faster end-to-end online training, and achieves statistically significant human-preference wins over PDD baselines (54.0% vs. 33.3% wins/losses, a 61.8% non-tie win rate, on RefVid-V4; 51.3% vs. 34.7%, a 59.7% non-tie win rate, on MiniMax-H3). They also report up to an 82.7% win rate against the naive DirectAlign approach.

**Tools & method (≤3 sentences):** BlockAlign constructs states at valid block boundaries and supervises "span-normalized transition residuals" against the fused block-level velocity predictor, supporting DPO-, NFT-, and rollout-style objectives while preserving the native few-step sampler. It is paired with a post-training pipeline combining offline preference pairs, reward-free semi-online continuations, and online rollouts, with hierarchical preference selection and objective-aware block scheduling.

**Limitation (≤3 sentences):** The authors note the method's cross-block guidance is only an "empirical approximation" that broadcasts a guidance direction across blocks without achieving exact multi-block credit assignment, and that an ablation on their scheduling choice was statistically inconclusive (p = 0.0709, confidence intervals including parity). They also report BlockAlign's automated aesthetic score (GPT-4o NaturalScore) came out slightly lower than baselines', which they partly attribute to baselines' stronger commercial-style aesthetics rather than a genuine quality deficit.

## The Flip Test: Video World Models Fail to Learn the World State
- **arXiv:** 2611.19822 · https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19822
- **Submitted:** 2026-11-15
- **Authors:** Mi Luo, Lijun Yu, Adams Wei Yu, Ang Cao, Baochen Sun, Ming-Hsuan Yang, Nan Hua, Yuan Hao, Alex Dimakis, Ya Xu, Ed H. Chi, Xuezhi Wang, Yinxiao Liu
- **Qualifying affiliation(s):** Google DeepMind — Lijun Yu, Adams Wei Yu, Ang Cao, Baochen Sun, Ming-Hsuan Yang, Nan Hua, Yuan Hao, Ya Xu, Ed H. Chi, Xuezhi Wang, Yinxiao Liu (Mi Luo: UT Austin, work done as a Google DeepMind intern; Alex Dimakis: UC Berkeley)
- **Categories:** cs.CV, cs.AI, cs.RO
- **Open release:** demo (project page https://flip-test.github.io/); no GitHub/HuggingFace link found
- **Shipped counterpart:** none found

**Summary (≤3 sentences):** The authors introduce FlipTest, a counterfactual benchmark that probes whether video world models actually track physical world state rather than just drawing plausible pixels. It uses minimal pairs where a tiny change to the initial frame (e.g., a box resting a few millimeters before or past a table edge) flips the true future outcome, across 1,680 frames from 10 simulated and 15 real-world tasks. All 11 evaluated video world models fail this test, performing at or below random chance.
**Purpose (≤3 sentences):** The paper asks whether current video world models encode genuine causal/physical state needed to predict divergent futures, as opposed to merely generating visually plausible continuations. It is motivated by the gap between "world models promise to predict the future" and whether that promise is actually kept.
**Breakthrough (≤3 sentences):** The authors report the best video model (Gemini Omni Flash 1.1) averages only 18.3% pair accuracy, below the 25% chance baseline, and that models flip their prediction about as often on steps that leave the future unchanged as on steps that change it. They also report that a vision-language model judging the same frames traces a near S-shaped decision curve near the physical boundary, and that a coding agent which measures pixel geometry comes close to the true boundary — leading the authors to conclude the deciding information is present in the frame but video models fail to extract it.
**Tools & method (≤3 sentences):** FlipTest has a three-level hierarchy: visual minimal pairs (detection), matched four-state controls (selectivity), and dense sweeps (localization of the model's implicit decision boundary). Outcomes are read off generated videos by two Gemini-based judges requiring agreement, and 11 models are tested, spanning general video generators (Gemini Omni Flash 1.1, MiniMax-H3, Wan2.2-I2V-A14B, HunyuanVideo-1.5, CogVideoX1.5-5B-I2V), physical-AI models (Cosmos-Predict2.5, Cosmos3-Nano/Super), and robotics predictors (VPP2, LingBot-Video-MoE-30B-A3B).
**Limitation (≤3 sentences):** The authors rely on VLM judges (two Gemini variants) whose agreement with human labels is reported at 79%, and the full limitations discussion (Appendix G) was not accessible in the fetched text. The evaluation set, while spanning simulated and real tasks, is still a curated set of physical-boundary scenarios rather than open-ended world dynamics.

## WA-JEPA: Goal-Aware Predictive Feedback Action Refinement for a World-Action Model
- **arXiv:** 2611.19183 · https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19183
- **Submitted:** 2026-11-14 (v1)
- **Authors:** Tao An, Weihang Liang, Tianqi Wang, Chuqing Cao, Wenming Tan, Kai Zhang, Haibo Lei, Tao Xie, Lijun Zhao, Ruifeng Li
- **Qualifying affiliation(s):** Hikvision Research Institute — Wenming Tan, Kai Zhang, Haibo Lei; FLAG: borderline — Hikvision (not on explicit company list; other co-authors are Harbin Institute of Technology and Anhui Polytechnic University)
- **Categories:** cs.CV
- **Open release:** code — https://github.com/Dovisya/WA-JEPA
- **Shipped counterpart:** none found

**Summary (≤3 sentences):** WA-JEPA is a JEPA-based "world-action" model that generates robot action proposals and then iteratively refines them using the model's own latent predictions of future scenes, instead of searching over candidate action sequences. It couples action generation, action-conditioned latent prediction, and two goal-aware feedback signals, and is evaluated on short-horizon, long-horizon, and full-task robotic manipulation benchmarks in simulation.
**Purpose (≤3 sentences):** Prior JEPA-based world models plan by exhaustively searching and re-evaluating candidate action sequences in latent space, which the authors say incurs substantial online compute cost. WA-JEPA aims to let predicted future latents directly refine a single action proposal instead, avoiding that search.
**Breakthrough (≤3 sentences):** The authors report WA-JEPA reaches 96% success on PushT, 100% on TwoRoom and Cube goal-reaching, and in long-horizon "Anchored Planning" it averages 80.21% (standard starts) and 66.28% (perturbed starts) success, 25.78 and 19.14 points above the LeWM baseline. On full-task execution it reports 71±2% PushT coverage and 97±1% LIBERO-Goal success, ahead of baselines such as Fast-WAM, RynnVLA-002 (96%), DreamVLA (86%) and Patch Policy (94%), while keeping a compact model and lower online compute.
**Tools & method (≤3 sentences):** The model generates an action proposal, predicts resulting future latent scenes/proprioceptive states, and refines the proposal via "goal-discrepancy residual feedback" (comparing predicted-scene and goal latents) and "future-scene context feedback" (preserving fine-grained predicted representations), trained jointly from offline demonstrations. Latency/compute figures were measured on a single NVIDIA A40 GPU at batch size one; all evaluation is in simulation (PushT, TwoRoom, Cube, LIBERO-Goal), with no physical robot used.
**Limitation (≤3 sentences):** The paper has no dedicated limitations section, but the authors note that in the hierarchical setting the speedup over the LeWM baseline shrinks because the baseline only searches a five-action chunk and both methods share retrieval/rendering overhead. They also note the goal-input ablation uses a single training seed and that early-exit inference latency was not measured.

## ResWAM: Learning What Changes, Not What Stays, for World-Action Models
- **arXiv:** 2611.19559 · https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19559
- **Submitted:** 2026-11-14
- **Authors:** Chongyu Fan, Gaowen Liu, Ramana Rao Kompella, Jason Corso, Sijia Liu
- **Qualifying affiliation(s):** FLAG: borderline — Cisco and IBM Research are listed among the paper's affiliations (alongside Michigan State University and University of Michigan); exact per-author mapping to Cisco/IBM was not clearly resolvable from the rendered HTML, but both corporate affiliations appear on the author block.
- **Categories:** cs.RO
- **Open release:** none found
- **Shipped counterpart:** none found

**Summary (≤3 sentences):** The paper addresses world-action models (WAMs) — vision-language-action policies augmented with future prediction — which the authors argue waste capacity supervising mostly-static full future observations instead of task-relevant motion. They propose "visual residual learning," supervising only the feature change between current and future frames in a frozen DINOv3 space, and build ResWAM, a 2.3B-parameter residual world-action model with a lightweight 0.5B dual-branch DiT trained via conditional flow matching.
**Purpose (≤3 sentences):** The goal is to make world-action models more sample- and compute-efficient by focusing the prediction objective on task-relevant robot/object motion rather than static background, and to reduce the heavy training/inference compute that existing WAMs (reliant on large video generation or separate world models) incur for closed-loop deployment.
**Breakthrough (≤3 sentences):** The authors report ResWAM achieves top average success rates on LIBERO-Plus (81.1%), RoboTwin 2.0 (93.8%), and RoboDojo (9.40% success, 13.41 score) without any robot pretraining, at the lowest training (2.7-4.0 TFLOPs) and inference compute (1.0-1.4 TFLOPs) among compared methods. On real-robot Open/Put/Stack tasks they report 80.0% average success (+10.0 points over π0.5) at 92.7ms inference latency.
**Tools & method (≤3 sentences):** ResWAM supervises only the DINOv3-space feature residual between current and future frames rather than the full future observation, which the authors report yields a higher feature rank and representations that spatially concentrate on the robot arm and manipulated objects rather than background. The architecture is a 2.3B-parameter model with a 0.5B lightweight dual-branch DiT jointly training an action branch and a residual branch via conditional flow matching.
**Limitation (≤3 sentences):** The authors' residual-target ablations are reported only on RoboTwin 2.0, and real-robot evaluation covers just three tasks (Open, Put, Stack). RoboDojo absolute success remains low (9.40%), and the approach depends on the choice of frozen feature encoder (DINOv3), with the paper's own ablation showing a +2.2 point gap versus using a VLM ViT residual target instead.

## Prepare Lighter and Faster for Open-Vocabulary Queries: A Query-Wise 3D Segmenter for Gaussian Splatting
- **arXiv:** 2611.19429 · https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19429

**Purpose (≤3 sentences):** The paper argues that existing game world models treat the video model merely as a restyling renderer of engine-authored motion, leaving most of its generative capacity unused and limiting game diversity. ## Découpage: Directorial Audio-Visual Control for Long Multi-Shot Video Generation
- **arXiv:** 2611.18866 · https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18866
- **Submitted:** 2026-11-13 (v1)
- **Authors:** Yifan Wang, Hao Ouyang, Qiuyu Wang, Yihao Meng, Wen Wang, Zeyue Tian, Yichong Lu, Hanlin Wang, Qingyan Bai, Zichen Liu, Yujun Shen, Qifeng Chen
- **Qualifying affiliation(s):** Ant Group (affiliation listed for the author group alongside HKUST and Zhejiang University; the HTML rendering lost the footnote markers linking individual names to institutions, so the specific Ant Group author(s) could not be individually confirmed); FLAG: borderline — Ant Group
- **Categories:** cs.AI (cross-listed cs.SD)
- **Open release:** demo — project page https://wyf0824.github.io/decoupage/ (authors state code will be released "upon publication," no link yet)
- **Shipped counterpart:** none found

**Summary (≤3 sentences):** DECOUPAGE is a screenplay-driven framework for generating long, multi-shot, multi-character videos with synchronized but independently-timed audio and visual tracks, addressing cinematic effects like dialogue that bridges across cuts (J-/L-cuts) and deliberate silences. It builds on a joint audio-video diffusion backbone with dual-schedule temporal cross-attention and a directorial lip-sync mechanism. It is evaluated on a self-constructed multi-shot conversational benchmark of 15- and 60-second screenplay-driven sequences.
**Purpose (≤3 sentences):** Existing audio-visual generative frameworks couple visual and audio timing by default and lack mechanisms to coordinate independent visual/acoustic timing over long, multi-shot narratives, causing them to hallucinate speech during intended silences or drive mouth movement on off-screen/silent listeners. DECOUPAGE aims to decouple generation into independently bounded visual and acoustic tracks governed by persistent character identities to fix this.
**Breakthrough (≤3 sentences):** On their 15-second benchmark the authors report Cut F1 of 0.99 versus 0.28 for the strongest baseline (MiniMax-H3), word error rate of 4.59% versus 10.23%, containment of 93.01% versus 41.95%, and silence leakage of 1.12% (best overall). On the harder 60-second benchmark they report Cut F1 of 0.76, containment of 91.12%, and silence leakage of 4.46%, versus 0.05 Cut F1 and 32.71% leakage for the LTX-2.3 Base baseline; an ablation shows removing dual-schedule temporal cross-attention drops Cut F1 from 0.99 to 0.34.
**Tools & method (≤3 sentences):** The system fine-tunes the LTX-2.3 Base joint audio-video diffusion backbone with LoRA (rank 32), adding dual-schedule temporal cross-attention and a directorial lip-sync mechanism that selectively decouples speech conditioning from visual facial dynamics during off-screen dialogue. Training used roughly 30,000 curated audio-video sequences; evaluation metrics include VBench Imaging Quality, AudioBox P-Q, SyncNet Sync-C, and a custom TemporalBias metric for listener lip coupling.
**Limitation (≤3 sentences):** The authors state their formulation "targets turn-taking dialogue and does not explicitly support simultaneous, overlapping speech," which limits performance in crowded group scenes where multiple characters speak at once. They note the lip-sync mechanism reduces listener TemporalBias (1.78 → 0.33) but adds roughly 0.20s per denoising step, and propose modeling overlapping speech as future work.
