## Training-Free Target-Aware Refinement of 3D Gaussian Language Map
- **arXiv:** 2610.21619 · https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21619
- **Submitted:** 2026-10-15 (v1, 10:00:53 UTC)
- **Authors:** Sibaek Lee, Kyeongsu Kang, Seongbo Ha, Yewon Jeon, Joonyeol Choi, Daegeol Ko, Jaehyuk Hur, Taemin Choi, Minsu Kim, Hyungjoo Ahn, Wonsok Yoo, Hyeonwoo Yu
- **Qualifying affiliation(s):** Samsung Electronics, Republic of Korea — Taemin Choi, Minsu Kim, Hyungjoo Ahn, Wonsok Yoo (remaining authors: Sungkyunkwan University). FLAG: borderline — Samsung Electronics is not on the core qualified list; kept per the "clearly comparable top-tier industry lab" rule.
- **Categories:** cs.RO (primary), cs.CV
- **Open release:** code/demo — project page states "Project page and code:" https://lab-of-ai-and-robotics.github.io/target-aware-langmap/
- **Shipped counterpart:** none found

**Summary (≤3 sentences):** The paper presents a training-free method to refine an already-distilled 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) language map so that a small set of user-specified text targets is retrieved reliably, without retraining the underlying map. It also identifies and corrects a shared-bias problem in the standard cosine query read-out.

**Purpose (≤3 sentences):** Existing open-vocabulary 3DGS language maps often fail to localize specific user-named targets reliably because of embedding biases in the distilled features. The authors aim to fix this at inference time, with no additional training and no per-scene hyperparameter tuning.

**Breakthrough (≤3 sentences):** The authors report the refinement roughly doubles base open-vocabulary segmentation mIoU on Replica (0.207 → 0.436) and raises top-1 instance-aware localization success from 44.6 to 76.8 on Replica. In a real-world legged-robot demo, they report a language-goal navigation success rate of 78% with the refined map versus 22% with the base map, and the method also lifts LangSplat's mIoU by 79–123% across three datasets as a plug-in.

**Tools & method (≤3 sentences):** A text-promptable segmenter (SAM 3) locates each target per view, and its visual embedding is blended toward the target's SigLIP 2 text embedding using a closed-form minimal blending coefficient (no tuning), on top of a SAM 2 + SigLIP 2 base map; a centering step at query read-out removes a shared bias the authors identify in injected embeddings. The method was demonstrated on a Unitree Go2 quadruped using LiDAR-inertial SLAM (LIO-SAM) and A* path planning on a 2D occupancy grid.

**Limitation (≤3 sentences):** The authors state that classes the segmenter never detects remain at zero IoU (the segmenter's recall is the bottleneck there), and that the minimal-overwrite strategy "may leave the most stubborn views unrecovered." They also note the object-level plug-in does not improve results on Replica because injection cannot create missing small-object instances.

## Speculative Forcing for Efficient Autoregressive Video Generation
- **arXiv:** 2610.22347 · https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22347
- **Submitted:** 2026-10-15 (v1, 17:57:01 UTC)
- **Authors:** Hyunwook Choi, Seungryong Kim, Dongyoon Han, Sangdoo Yun
- **Qualifying affiliation(s):** NAVER AI Lab — Dongyoon Han, Sangdoo Yun (Hyunwook Choi did the work during a NAVER AI Lab internship; Seungryong Kim is KAIST AI). **FLAG: borderline** (NAVER AI Lab is not on the default qualified-company list; it is South Korea's largest internet/tech company and runs an established industry AI research lab, included here per the skill's "comparable top-tier lab, flag if unsure" rule). Note: the screening lead expected an NVIDIA co-author — this is **not confirmed**; NVIDIA appears only in the reference list (a cited world-simulation paper) and as the GPU used for benchmarking (one NVIDIA H100), not as an author affiliation.
- **Categories:** cs.CV
- **Open release:** demo/project page only (speculative-forcing.github.io); no code repository found
- **Shipped counterpart:** none found

**Summary (≤3 sentences):** The paper addresses inefficiency in few-step autoregressive video generators, which run every token through the full denoising schedule even after that token's content has effectively converged. Speculative Forcing treats each intermediate clean estimate as a draft and uses a verifier to decide when a token can be safely accepted and frozen early, while keeping its cached keys/values as context for later tokens. The authors report this keeps quality close to the full-step baseline while increasing throughput across short-video, long-video, and world-generation settings.
**Purpose (≤3 sentences):** Few-step autoregressive video diffusion models are already more efficient than full diffusion, but the paper argues they still waste computation by denoising every token to the same fixed depth regardless of how quickly it has stabilized. The goal is adaptive, per-token compute allocation without retraining the base generator from scratch or sacrificing output quality.
**Breakthrough (≤3 sentences):** On LongLive (5-second videos) the authors report their learned verifier reaches a VBench-style total score of 83.21 (vs. 83.08 for the 4-step baseline) at 41.74 FPS (vs. 26.40 FPS), using about 1.68 mean denoising steps per token. On LongLive 30-second videos they report 82.81 vs. 82.69 at 37.8 vs. 24.5 FPS. On Lingbot-World 2.0 (world generation) they report a 1.35x speedup to 24.5 FPS at about 2.55 mean steps per token, versus 18.1 FPS for the 4-step baseline.
**Tools & method (≤3 sentences):** Two verifiers are proposed: a training-free one based on similarity-to-clean-content and step-to-step stability, and a learned one that predicts each token's remaining gap to the full-step output from the frozen generator's intermediate features. Evaluation used VBench (16 dimensions) and VBench-Long with Self-Forcing-rewritten prompts, VidProM prompts for training the LongLive verifier, and about 1K initial images plus a held-out 200-scene navigation benchmark (camera-pose RPE and a drift metric) for the Lingbot verifier. Throughput was measured on a single NVIDIA H100 GPU.
**Limitation (≤3 sentences):** The authors state the achievable speedup is generator- and domain-dependent: sensitive backbones and harder domains such as world generation require more denoising steps, so the method does not guarantee the same acceleration across generators or domains. They also note the clean KV-refresh pass adds overhead, so realized speedup does not scale proportionally with mean denoising depth because each chunk runs until its last active token is accepted.

## Seeing Both the Forest and the Trees: Reusing Holistic 3D Priors for Part-Decomposed Generation
- **arXiv:** 2610.21850 · https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21850
- **Submitted:** 2026-10-15 (v1, 13:27:34 UTC)
- **Authors:** Yiqun Zhao, Binbin Huang, Haobin Duan, Zibo Zhao, Shenghua Gao
- **Qualifying affiliation(s):** Tencent — Zibo Zhao (other co-authors at The University of Hong Kong, Transengram, and Shenzhen Loop Area Institute). Not borderline. Note: Alibaba, also flagged by screening, is **not** an author affiliation — it appears only in a Figure 1 citation ("Z-Image Team, Alibaba Group (2025)") referring to an image generator used to create input images.
- **Categories:** cs.CV (primary), cs.GR
- **Open release:** demo/project page only (https://zhao-yiqun.github.io/structparts/); no code repository found
- **Shipped counterpart:** none found

**Summary (≤3 sentences):** The paper introduces STRUCTPARTS, a method for part-decomposed 3D generation from a single image that aims to preserve both fine intra-part geometric detail and structural coherence across an object's parts. It interprets each shape latent in both a part-local and an object-global reference frame, processed by a global-local interleaved transformer, and takes a segmented mesh scaffold (a coarse mesh plus face-level segmentation) as input. The authors report it matches or surpasses prior part-decomposed models on PartObjaverse-Tiny at a fraction of their training cost.
**Purpose (≤3 sentences):** Existing image-conditioned 3D generators capture both detail and coherence but output a single fused mesh, while part-decomposed methods that adapt holistic 3D priors tend to disrupt those priors. STRUCTPARTS aims to reuse a pretrained holistic 3D generator's weights, via lightweight finetuning, to produce both per-part detail and whole-object coherence together.
**Breakthrough (≤3 sentences):** On PartObjaverse-Tiny part-level metrics the authors report CD 0.107 / F@0.05 0.67 / F@0.10 0.77 for StructParts, versus 0.114/0.63/0.75 for a released X-Part baseline and 0.169/0.55/0.69 for an X-Part variant retrained at matched compute budget. On a 200-object ABO cross-dataset test they report 0.021 mean CD and 0.68 F@0.05, close to released X-Part's 0.023 and 0.68. An ablation shows part-level CD degrades from 0.107 to 0.331 when the dual-frame design is replaced with local-RoPE-only.
**Tools & method (≤3 sentences):** The "dual-frame coordinate interleaving" mechanism alternates intra-part and inter-part attention over latents interpreted in both local and global coordinate frames, built by lightweight finetuning of the pretrained holistic model Hunyuan3D-2.1. Training used Partverse-XL (about 36K objects, about 240K parts) on 8 H200 GPUs for about 4 days (768 H200-hours, 40K steps); evaluation used PartObjaverse-Tiny (200 objects) and a 200-object ABO subset.
**Limitation (≤3 sentences):** The authors state performance depends on the accuracy of the input segmented mesh scaffold, and the method "does not reliably correct inaccurate scaffolds," so scaffold errors can propagate into generated parts. They also note their robustness tests measure only local sensitivity to controlled perturbations, not the full range of natural scaffold failures, and list deeper part hierarchies and articulated objects as future work.

## SUPRA-TEX: 3D Generation with Super-Aligned Texture
- **arXiv:** 2610.22352 · https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22352
- **Submitted:** 2026-10-15 (v1, 17:58:02 UTC)
- **Authors:** Chen Yang, Jiemin Fang, Fanpeng Meng, Zihao Huang, Jichen Pang, Guanjun Wu, Zhikuan Bao, Qi Tian
- **Qualifying affiliation(s):** Huawei Inc. — Chen Yang, Jiemin Fang, Fanpeng Meng, Zihao Huang, Jichen Pang, Guanjun Wu, Zhikuan Bao, Qi Tian (additional academic affiliations: Shanghai Jiao Tong University, Huazhong University of Science and Technology); **FLAG: borderline** (Huawei). Note: screening had flagged "Tencent Hunyuan3D Team" as a possible co-author affiliation — verified this is **false**; Tencent Hunyuan3D only appears as a cited baseline/related-work comparison (Hunyuan3D 2.1), not as an author affiliation. The real affiliation on this paper is Huawei Inc.
- **Categories:** cs.CV (primary), cs.GR
- **Open release:** weights, code (paper states "Code and checkpoints are released on our project page" at supra-3d.github.io/supratex)
- **Shipped counterpart:** none found

**Summary (≤3 sentences):** SUPRA-Tex is a single-image 3D texturing method that estimates relightable materials (albedo, metallic-roughness) for the visible surface of a given mesh, aligns them precisely to that surface, and completes unobserved regions. It separates material estimation, alignment, and completion into distinct stages instead of treating texturing as one fused step. The authors report state-of-the-art results on geometry-conditioned texturing and end-to-end asset generation using only public training data.
**Purpose (≤3 sentences):** The paper targets a failure mode of prior single-image 3D generators: fine reference details such as text, logos, and patterns are not faithfully reproduced at their correct visible-surface locations, and hidden regions are completed inconsistently. It aims to produce textures with "super-aligned" fidelity to the input image while also handling lighting separation (relightable materials) and completeness for unseen areas.
**Breakthrough (≤3 sentences):** The authors report shaded PSNR of 30.89/30.05 (DTC-Tex/Complex-Tex) for given-geometry texturing versus 29.19/28.84 for the Pixal3D⋆ baseline, and LPIPS of 0.025/0.029 versus 0.071/0.070 for the same baseline in the end-to-end setting. In a 45-image human pairwise-preference comparison against five commercial systems, the authors report win rates of 51.1% to 84.8% for their method, and a VLM PBR score of 8.67 (tied for highest). They also report sampling about 1.18–1.20x faster than joint-update attention.
**Tools & method (≤3 sentences):** An MLLM-based image editing model predicts lighting-separated material maps (albedo, metallic-roughness) from the single reference view; these are projected onto visible surface voxels and encoded into "native observation latents." An interleaved flow transformer treats these latents as fixed, read-only anchors while generating the complete material field for occluded regions. Training used 64 GPUs with a global batch size of 576; the 2D material estimator runs in about 1.2s per inference on one H800 GPU, and runtime benchmarks use one H800 per request. Evaluation used DTC-Tex (100 Digital Twin Catalog product scans) and Complex-Tex (110 Sketchfab meshes).
**Limitation (≤3 sentences):** The authors state that large discrepancies between the input image and the upstream geometry can misplace observations, since the geometry stays fixed during material generation, and that upstream geometry can hallucinate unseen structure. They note text or patterns that wrap past the visible region may be completed inconsistently, and the 2D material estimator can misassign properties (e.g., high metallic values on non-metallic surfaces).

## Motion- and Gravity-Aware Feedforward 3D Reconstruction
- **arXiv:** 2610.22177 · https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22177
- **Submitted:** 2026-10-15 (v1, 16:34:04 UTC)
- **Authors:** Shiyao Qian, Yuan Ren, Dongfeng Bai
- **Qualifying affiliation(s):** Huawei Noah's Ark Lab, Markham, Canada — Yuan Ren, Dongfeng Bai (first author Shiyao Qian is University of Toronto, with the work done during an internship at Huawei Noah's Ark Lab); **FLAG: borderline** (Huawei).
- **Categories:** cs.CV (primary), cs.AI
- **Open release:** none found
- **Shipped counterpart:** none found

**Summary (≤3 sentences):** The paper targets feed-forward multi-view 3D reconstruction transformers (e.g., VGGT, π³), which the authors say rely mainly on appearance-based attention and struggle in dynamic scenes with weak geometric cues. They propose adding residual optical flow and gravity direction as privileged training signals under the Learning Using Privileged Information (LUPI) paradigm, while inference uses only standard visual inputs. The authors report accuracy and robustness gains, particularly for dynamic objects, ambiguous structure, and difficult camera motion.
**Purpose (≤3 sentences):** Feed-forward reconstruction transformers perform well on static, well-textured scenes but the authors state they struggle when geometric cues are weak or scenes are dynamic. The paper's purpose is to inject motion and gravity priors during training only, so no extra inputs or compute are needed at inference.
**Breakthrough (≤3 sentences):** In the paper's 8-view setting (Table 2), the authors report residual-flow injection improves π³ depth-rel to 0.107 (from 0.116 without injection) and VGGT depth-rel to 0.114 (from 0.121). The best reported configuration overall is gravity injected at a late output stage for π³ (depth-rel 0.095, rays-err 1.478, pose AUC 49.39, depth inliers 46.39). The authors state the best injection stage is backbone-dependent: middle layers for VGGT, late-stage for π³.
**Tools & method (≤3 sentences):** Residual optical flow is computed by subtracting a global affine motion model from RAFT optical flow; gravity direction is the second privileged signal. A two-stage finetuning schedule is used — privileged feature alignment, then knowledge distillation with modality dropout — applied as lightweight architectural additions to VGGT and π³. Training used 11 datasets (BlendedMVS, Mapillary Planet-Scale Depth, Spring, UnrealStereo4K, Aria Synthetic Environments, DL3DV-10K, Dynamic Replica, MegaDepth, MVS-Synth, ParallelDomain-4D, SAIL-VOS 3D) with benchmarking on ETH3D and TartanAirV2-WB; hardware was not stated.
**Limitation (≤3 sentences):** The authors note that for π³, residual-flow injection actually has a lower Pose AUC (40.94) than no injection (42.14) on the 8-view benchmark, so the gain is not universal across metrics. They state that rigid optical flow does not provide a consistent notion of camera dynamics across unordered view sets, limiting its benefit, and suggest future work on streaming/temporally coherent settings with explicit ego-motion modeling.

## GeoState: Learning View-Decoupled Geometry States for 3D World Modeling
- **arXiv:** 2610.22343 · https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22343
- **Submitted:** 2026-10-15 (v1, 17:56:18 UTC)
- **Authors:** Hongchang Liu, Jian Liu, Yujuan Ding, Liang Peng, Xun Jiang, Fei Ma, Xing Xu, Yi Bin
- **Qualifying affiliation(s):** Tencent — Liang Peng (co-authors otherwise at Tongji University, Hong Kong Polytechnic University, and Guangdong Laboratory of Artificial Intelligence and Digital Economy). Not borderline. Note: NVIDIA, also flagged by screening, is **not** an author affiliation — it appears only in the reference list as the author of a cited Cosmos world-foundation-model paper.
- **Categories:** cs.CV (primary), cs.AI, cs.LG, cs.RO
- **Open release:** demo/project page only (https://hongchang-liu.github.io/GeoState-project-page/); no code repository found
- **Shipped counterpart:** none found

**Summary (≤3 sentences):** GeoState proposes a view-decoupled scene-state representation for predictive 3D world modeling, built to avoid entangling scene dynamics with camera-motion-induced changes in geometry-foundation-model features. A Perceiver-style tokenizer aggregates multi-view VGGT-Ω features into a fixed-size state, which a query-conditioned geometry decoder reads out via camera rays, while an autoregressive world model predicts future states. The authors report GeoState outperforms prior 3D world models on Waymo and generalizes zero-shot to KITTI and nuScenes.
**Purpose (≤3 sentences):** Geometry foundation model (GFM) features are organized around the observing viewpoint, so camera motion changes the features even when the underlying scene is static, which the authors say entangles scene dynamics with camera-induced variation and makes forecasting harder. GeoState is designed to decouple these two factors by maintaining a state representation independent of query viewpoint.
**Breakthrough (≤3 sentences):** On Waymo depth forecasting with predicted cameras, the authors report AbsRel/δ1 of 0.065/0.939 at t+0.2s, 0.092/0.902 at t+1.0s, and 0.127/0.848 at t+2.0s for GeoState, versus 0.073/0.931, 0.143/0.826, and 0.213/0.731 for VGGT-World — a reported 40.4% lower AbsRel at t+2.0s. Zero-shot on KITTI at t+2.0s they report 0.155/0.821 for GeoState versus 0.198/0.732 for VGGT-World, and report similar gains on all 150 nuScenes validation scenes.
**Tools & method (≤3 sentences):** The scene tokenizer aggregates multi-view VGGT-Ω features into a 512×512 state; a query-conditioned geometry decoder uses Plücker rays from a query camera and reuses the frozen VGGT-Ω depth head; an autoregressive residual world model predicts future states from a four-state context with seven-step rollout during training. Pretraining used about 9.5k scenes from DL3DV, TartanAir, RealEstate10K, CO3D, and WildRGB-D; Waymo adaptation used about 139k windows from 798 segments (100 held out for evaluation); zero-shot evaluation used 113 KITTI drives and all 150 nuScenes validation scenes. Hardware was not reported.
**Limitation (≤3 sentences):** The authors report that latent matching throughout the rollout (versus one-step state supervision) hurts long-horizon accuracy, which they attribute to GFM feature variations that do not always reflect true geometric change. They note the method relies on VGGT-Ω and its centered-principal-point intrinsics assumption, worked around with dataset-calibrated intrinsics, and that adaptation uses only a static Waymo subset; extension to embodied settings is listed as future work.

## GRASE: Geometry-native Real-to-sim Agentic Scene Modeling from Video
- **arXiv:** 2610.21498 · https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21498
- **Submitted:** 2026-10-15 (v1, 08:34:00 UTC)
- **Authors:** Zhaorui Lin, Haohan Liu, Zhen Chen, Xiangru Huang
- **Qualifying affiliation(s):** Adobe Systems — Zhen Chen (other authors: Xi'an Jiaotong University, Fuzhou University, Westlake University)
- **Categories:** cs.CV
- **Open release:** none found (no Comments field, project page, or code link on the abstract page)
- **Shipped counterpart:** none found

**Summary (≤3 sentences):** GRASE is an agentic framework that turns a captured multi-view video of a real indoor space into an editable, simulation-ready 3D scene, treating the original video as a persistent reference it checks against throughout. It fits the room's structural shell and openings, places generated assets in a shared coordinate frame, and iteratively refines each asset's geometry and placement.

**Purpose (≤3 sentences):** The authors target error accumulation in one-pass real-to-sim pipelines, where mistakes made in early stages (layout fitting, object placement) propagate uncorrected into the final scene. GRASE instead keeps revising its scene hypothesis whenever rendered views disagree with the captured video.

**Breakthrough (≤3 sentences):** On the authors' Imaginarium benchmark (15 scenes) and 8 Replica scenes, the authors report GRASE substantially lowers scene-level Chamfer Distance versus the strongest baseline (e.g., Imaginarium: 3.14 cm vs. 11.00 cm for GPT-6 + TRELLIS.2 + point cloud) and raises object instance mIoU (e.g., Imaginarium: 0.74 vs. 0.21). They report it outperforms SAM3D and GPT-6-based real-to-sim baselines, including one built on "GPT6-Astra."

**Tools & method (≤3 sentences):** The pipeline samples video frames, runs Depth Anything 3 and SAM 3 for depth/segmentation, fits the room shell with RANSAC and parametric patches for curved surfaces, and places/generates objects via TRELLIS.2 or Blender-MCP-built primitives. A tool-using VLM agent iteratively verifies rendered views against the captured video and accepts only edits that reduce the diagnosed discrepancy without regressions.

**Limitation (≤3 sentences):** The authors acknowledge reconstruction is limited in heavily occluded regions with insufficient geometric evidence, which are completed by extending surfaces or generative priors and marked as inferred rather than observed. They also note the inventory/support-graph/verification steps depend on VLM judgments (so early errors can persist under ambiguous evidence), and that the method assumes a static scene, not recovering articulation, materials, or physical properties.

## Compression of Uniformly Distributed Unit Quaternions
- **arXiv:** 2610.22052 · https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22052
- **Submitted:** 2026-10-15 (v1, 15:19:24 UTC)
- **Authors:** Carsten Faber, Bastian Kuth, Rinaldo Schuster, Daniel Pauls, Quirin Meyer
- **Qualifying affiliation(s):** AMD, Germany — Bastian Kuth, Quirin Meyer (other co-authors at Coburg University of Applied Sciences and Arts, Germany); **FLAG: borderline** (AMD).
- **Categories:** cs.GR
- **Open release:** code (reference C implementation at https://github.com/GeoFlowResearch/UnitQuaternionCompressionSupplemental)
- **Shipped counterpart:** none found

**Summary (≤3 sentences):** The paper compares parameterizations for compressing uniformly distributed unit quaternions, a problem relevant to reducing memory and bandwidth for rotations in computer graphics. The authors propose ℓ∞cub, an infinity-norm parameterization with an optimized cubic warp function, mixed-radix packing, and range quantization, designed as a drop-in replacement for existing quaternion codecs. They report it gives the smallest maximum error and best storage efficiency among the compared methods, with decompression cost equal to or lower than common existing methods.
**Purpose (≤3 sentences):** Unit quaternions are the standard representation for 3D rotations in graphics (skeletal animation, tangent-space normal mapping, Gaussian splatting) but consume significant memory at scale. The paper's purpose is a quaternion codec that minimizes worst-case angular error per bit while keeping decode cost low enough for real-time use.
**Breakthrough (≤3 sentences):** The authors report that at 32 bits, ℓ∞cub achieves a compactness factor of 135°, 13.4% below the ℓ∞tan baseline. In skeletal animation (Mixamo, 34.9M keyframes) at 48 bits, they report a maximum error of 0.00203° versus 0.00909° for Ozz's ℓ2I codec (about 5.6x lower) with 12–16% faster sampling. In Gaussian splatting on three Mip-NeRF 360 scenes at 16 bits, they report 41.68 dB PSNR versus 35.01 dB for an exponential-map codec.
**Tools & method (≤3 sentences):** ℓ∞cub projects quaternions via an ℓ∞ norm, applies a cubic warp with one optimized parameter (a* ≈ 0.373964), and uses mixed-radix packing with range quantization. Evaluation used the Mixamo animation corpus, Khronos glTF Sample Assets, Poly Haven, and Smithsonian Open Access (tangent frames: 397k frames across 14 glTF assets), plus Mip-NeRF 360 scenes (bicycle, garden, room) and a demon dragon model for Gaussian splatting and skinning renders. Hardware used was an AMD Radeon RX 7900 XTX (most experiments, including HIP decode-cycle measurements) and an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4070 Ti (Gaussian splatting experiments).
**Limitation (≤3 sentences):** The authors state the analysis assumes uniformly distributed quaternions and does not adapt the coding range per track or segment, so concentrated rotation sets can be coded more compactly by other methods. They note the closed-form first-order error is derived for ℓ∞tan but not for ℓ∞cub, that range quantization is more complex to encode than simple rounding (though decoding stays O(1)), and that identity rotations are reconstructed exactly only with an odd number of quantization states.

## Anchor3DGS: Feed-Forward 3D Gaussian Splatting with Compact Anchor-Based Representation
- **arXiv:** 2610.21634 · https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21634
- **Submitted:** 2026-10-15 (v1, 10:18:41 UTC)
- **Authors:** Sheng Ye, Jiangke Lin, Fudong Wang, Yi Yuan, Zhen-Hui Dong, Yong-Jin Liu
- **Qualifying affiliation(s):** Ant Group, Inclusion AI — Sheng Ye, Jiangke Lin, Fudong Wang, Yi Yuan (Sheng Ye is also Tsinghua University; Zhen-Hui Dong and Yong-Jin Liu are Tsinghua University only). **FLAG: borderline** (Ant Group — not Alibaba Group; these are distinct, separately-operating companies. Screening had expected an Alibaba co-author; the real, verified affiliation is Ant Group / Inclusion AI, which does not appear in the default qualified-company list but is included here per the skill's "comparable top-tier lab, flag if unsure" rule given Ant Group's scale and its Inclusion AI research org).
- **Categories:** cs.CV
- **Open release:** none found
- **Shipped counterpart:** none found

**Summary (≤3 sentences):** Anchor3DGS is a feed-forward 3D Gaussian Splatting method that does not require known camera poses and replaces the common one-Gaussian-per-pixel design with a compact, fixed budget of 3D anchors. Anchors are placed using entropy-guided sampling so they concentrate in visually complex regions while still covering the scene, each aggregating multi-view features with occlusion awareness before decoding into local Gaussians. The authors report state-of-the-art novel-view-synthesis quality using roughly an order of magnitude fewer Gaussian primitives than prior feed-forward methods.
**Purpose (≤3 sentences):** Pixel-aligned feed-forward 3DGS methods tie the number of Gaussians to input image resolution and view count, which the authors say does not scale well. The goal is a fixed-size, compact scene representation that keeps feed-forward speed while reducing redundant primitives.
**Breakthrough (≤3 sentences):** With an anchor budget of N=32,768 anchors and K=8 Gaussians per anchor (about 0.26M Gaussians per scene), the authors report 3-view PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS of 23.69/0.764/0.320 on VR-NeRF versus 21.31/0.729/0.340 for the AnySplat baseline, and 21.36/0.678/0.307 vs. 15.65/0.624/0.331 on MipNeRF360. In dense-view (32-view) tests they report comparable or better quality than AnySplat while using about 0.26M Gaussians versus AnySplat's 4.75M–3.98M. They also report Co3Dv2 pose-estimation AUC@30 of 81.5, versus 80.8 for AnySplat and 74.9 for VGGT. Note: the paper's own Section 5.2 text claims gains "over 22 dB," which the tables do not support (actual gains are roughly 2–6 dB) — this appears to be an error in the paper, not a verified result.
**Tools & method (≤3 sentences):** Anchors are placed on a VGGT-based geometry scaffold via entropy-guided sampling, aggregate multi-view image features with occlusion-aware weighting, exchange information via self-attention, and decode into local Gaussians; a lightweight UNet refines the splatted features into an RGB residual. Training used DL3DV, ScanNet++, Hypersim, BlendedMVS, and Waymo on 8 NVIDIA H20 GPUs for 30,000 iterations (roughly 22–33 days); evaluation used VR-NeRF, MipNeRF360, held-out DL3DV, and Co3Dv2 for pose, with dense-view inference timed on a single H800 and FPS measured on a single H20.
**Limitation (≤3 sentences):** The authors state that method performance "relies on the prediction quality of the geometry backbone," so inaccurate poses or noisy depth can degrade anchor placement and rendering. They also note the fixed anchor budget may not suit very large or unbounded scenes, leaving a scene-adaptive budget to future work.

## WorldCast: Exploring Intelligent Visual Generation in World State Prediction
- **arXiv:** 2610.20731 · https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20731

**Summary (≤3 sentences):** WorldCast is a 320-case, 8-category benchmark for multi-step "world state prediction" — testing whether visual generative models can produce the correct sequence of future visual states given a scenario, beyond basic text-to-image/video generation. It sits at the top (L3/L4) of a four-level capability hierarchy the authors propose for multimodal generation.

**Purpose (≤3 sentences):** The authors argue that existing benchmarks mostly test simpler generation (static text-to-image, editing, basic video) and that the highest-level tasks — dynamic, physically plausible transitions guided by visual/physical/procedural references — are underexplored. WorldCast is built to probe this gap across 1,352 requested visual states.

**Breakthrough (≤3 sentences):** The authors report evaluating 12 models and finding frontier proprietary image models advancing quickly (GPT-Image-2.5 scores 72.8 overall) while substantial gaps remain versus other systems (Nano Banana Pro 42.8, best open-source model SenseNova-U1 at 20.1; best video model Seedance 2.0 at 47.2). They also report that stronger underlying language-model reasoning correlates with better generative world-state prediction performance.

**Tools & method (≤3 sentences):** Scoring combines Task Completion, Causal Plausibility, and Visual Consistency (weighted 0.4/0.4/0.2) using GPT-5.5 as an LMM-as-a-judge, with "GPT-6 Astra" (via Codex) as an agentic video adapter that extracts keyframes from generated videos for scoring. The authors report judge-vs-human agreement of Pearson r ≥ 0.97 across four validation dimensions, and that rescoring with other judges (GLM-5.3-Flash, Gemini 3.1 Pro) preserved the model ranking.

**Limitation (≤3 sentences):** The paper has no dedicated limitations section; the authors note video world-model benchmarks were deliberately excluded because they don't test the reasoning-based transitions and heterogeneous references WorldCast targets. They suggest distilling expensive code-based simulation into direct visual generation as future work, and state no release of the benchmark, code, or data.

## DeSpatial: The devil of Agentic Spatial Reasoning Lies in 3D Itself
- **arXiv:** 2610.20990 · https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20990
- **Submitted:** 2026-10-14 (v1, 22:05:23 UTC)
- **Authors:** Yifei Liu, Xiaolong Zhou, Ziyang Gong, Yifan Zhou, Yuanyuan Gao, Yuning Gong, Yuchen Yang, Hongjie Zhang, Xue Yang, Xiao Sun, Zhihang Zhong

Other authors: Shanghai Artificial Intelligence Laboratory, Beihang University.
- **Categories:** cs.CV
- **Open release:** none found
- **Shipped counterpart:** none found

**Summary (≤3 sentences):** DeSpatial splits agentic spatial reasoning into a shared 3D perception stage — building one unified, metric, gravity-aligned scene with consistent object identities — and a separate reasoning stage that operates on that scene. The authors argue, and verified on-topic by close reading, that this is a 3D scene-reconstruction and -understanding paper, not a generic agentic-reasoning benchmark paper: its core contribution is a perception pipeline that fuses segmentation and depth into consistent 3D geometry.

**Purpose (≤3 sentences):** The paper addresses the problem that spatial agents combine independently predicted tools (segmentation, depth, detection) whose outputs disagree with each other, producing fragmented or duplicated object identities that corrupt downstream spatial answers. The authors set out to show that unreliable 3D perception, not reasoning, is the main bottleneck.

**Breakthrough (≤3 sentences):** In an oracle test, the authors report that swapping ground-truth 3D bounding boxes into the agent raises accuracy on five object-centric VSI-Bench tasks from 50.38% (raw perception pipeline) to 98.23%. With DeSpatial's own perception pipeline (no ground truth), they report 69.22% average on full VSI-Bench, 87.19% on MindCube-Tiny, 68.35% on ViewSpatial, and recall of 86.7–90.3% across time windows up to 240 minutes on VSI-SUPER, versus baselines such as S-Agent (75.0–77.2% recall) and Cambrian-S-7B-LFP (40.0–45.0%).

**Tools & method (≤3 sentences):** A joint segmentation-depth co-refinement module merges same-object masks across a frame and removes projection artifacts, and a cross-view instance fusion module links overlapping instances of the same object across different views (not within one frame) using 3D structure and mask-appearance checks. Results use a Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct backbone with SAM3, Pi3, Depth Anything 3, VGGT, and GeoCalib as the perception tools.

**Limitation (≤3 sentences):** The authors state the unified representation depends on detection quality, reconstructed geometry, metric scale, and gravity estimates, so it cannot recover unobserved surfaces or missing detections, and incorrect part-count or identity judgments can discard valid geometry or wrongly merge distinct objects. They also note the oracle result covers only five object-centric categories with annotation-consistent answers, so it does not show perception is the only bottleneck across all spatial tasks.
