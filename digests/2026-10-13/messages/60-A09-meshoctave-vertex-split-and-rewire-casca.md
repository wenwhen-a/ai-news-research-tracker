**[A09] MeshOctave: Vertex Split-and-Rewire Cascades for Native Mesh Generation**
- **arXiv:** 2609.38985 · <https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38985>
- **Submitted:** 2026-09-30
- **Authors:** Junkai Lin et al.
- **Qualifying affiliation(s):** Meshy AI (several authors, including the corresponding-adjacent "Project Leader" marker and a footnote stating one author's contribution "was done while interning at Meshy AI"). **Flag: BORDERLINE** — Meshy AI is a 3D-generation startup, not on the main tracked-company list (other co-authors: Huazhong University of Science and Technology, Technical University of Munich, and an independent researcher).
- **Categories:** cs.CV

**Summary:** Current artist-style mesh generation methods either generate connectivity tokens one at a time (slow) or rely on heuristic connectivity decoders.
**Purpose:** To generate artist-quality 3D meshes natively (with proper topology/connectivity) faster and more faithfully than token-by-token autoregressive or heuristic-decoder approaches.
**Breakthrough:** The authors report MeshOctave beats all compared autoregressive, next-scale, and flow-matching baselines on every metric in their main comparison (CD-L2 0.0392 vs. 0.0406 for the strongest baseline; Hausdorff distance 0.0645 vs. 0.0665; normal consistency 0.8478 vs. 0.8333).
**Tools & method:** A scale-conditioned masked discrete diffusion model trained on ~350K meshes filtered from Objaverse and Toys4K (up to 15K faces); evaluated on 300 meshes from ObjaverseXL/Toys4K plus 200 meshes generated by large 3D generative models.
**Limitation:** Sequential generation across scales has higher latency than single-scale flow matching, and because the method has no backtracking, coarse topological errors can propagate to finer scales.
