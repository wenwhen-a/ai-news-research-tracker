## MotionInsight: Diagnosing Object Motion Deficiencies in Generated Videos
- **arXiv:** 2609.37030 · https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37030
- **Submitted:** 2026-09-29
- **Authors:** Jiahao Zhan, Yongrui Ma, Qunliang Xing, Xuanyu Zhang, Jingqi Tong, Junlin Li, Li Zhang, Shijie Zhao, Tianfan Xue
- **Qualifying affiliation(s):** ByteDance Inc. — Jiahao Zhan, Yongrui Ma, Qunliang Xing, Junlin Li, Li Zhang, Shijie Zhao (project lead); other authors from MMLab CUHK, Peking University, Fudan University, CPII under InnoHK
- **Categories:** cs.CV; cs.AI
- **Open release:** code — https://github.com/JohnZhan2023/MotionInsight (paper states "the code is publicly available")
- **Shipped counterpart:** none found

**Summary (≤3 sentences):** The paper introduces MotionInsight, an evaluator that diagnoses object-motion deficiencies (object consistency, motion continuity, physical plausibility) in AI-generated videos, paired with the VidMotion dataset of 6,879 annotated videos with 12 failure-cause categories. The evaluator extracts motion embeddings from point tracking (CoTracker3) and camera pose (ViPE) rather than relying on raw RGB frames, and is trained with GRPO using motion-specific rewards for human-preference alignment and diagnostic explanation.
**Purpose (≤3 sentences):** Existing video-generation evaluation focuses on aesthetics and text-video alignment while ignoring whether generated object motion is physically and temporally realistic; the authors aim to close that gap with an object-centric, explainable motion-fidelity evaluator.
**Breakthrough (≤3 sentences):** The authors report MotionInsight reaches near-human correlation with human judgments (SRCC 0.761/0.633/0.727 on the three dimensions, versus 0.370/0.276/0.246 for GPT-5.4) and a Jaccard similarity of 0.58 for failure-cause identification versus 0.15 for GPT-5.4. They also report that using MotionInsight as a DPO reward signal yields videos preferred 92.86% of the time over a baseline Wan 2.1 model for motion fidelity.
**Tools & method (≤3 sentences):** The system combines CoTracker3 point tracking and ViPE camera-pose estimation into structured "motion embeddings," aligns them to a VLM's semantic space using 80,721 auto-generated motion-description QA pairs from OpenVid, and fine-tunes with GRPO on 8 NVIDIA A100 GPUs. VidMotion videos were generated across 9 open and proprietary models (Wan 2.2/2.6, LTX 2.1, LongCat-Video, Cosmos-Predict 2.5, HunyuanVideo-1.5, Sora 2, Veo 3.1, Seedance 2.0).
**Limitation (≤3 sentences):** The authors state the object-centric scoring is "less suitable for dynamic phenomena such as fluids, smoke, fire, splashes, or highly deformable materials." They also flag a Goodhart's-law risk when using the fixed evaluator as an optimization reward, since it "does not automatically evolve with advancing generators" and could be gamed over time.

## MeteoVerse: Unified Weather-Controllable Video World Model
- **arXiv:** 2609.36810 · https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36810
- **Submitted:** 2026-09-29
- **Authors:** Renlong Wu, Guanqiao Wang, Xuan Shang, Yin Hanming, Xiaoxiao Sheng, Tianyu Huang, Hui Li, Wangmeng Zuo
- **Qualifying affiliation(s):** Huawei — Xiaoxiao Sheng, Tianyu Huang; other authors from Harbin Institute of Technology. FLAG: borderline
- **Categories:** cs.CV
- **Open release:** none (project page only: https://meteoverse.github.io/; no code or weights release stated in the fetched text)
- **Shipped counterpart:** none found

**Summary (≤3 sentences):** MeteoVerse is a video world model that generates future video conditioned on scene description, weather instruction, and camera path, explicitly modeling weather transitions (preserving, introducing, or removing rain/snow/fog with intensity control) rather than leaving them implicit. It pairs a LoRA-fine-tuned Qwen3-VL-2B weather-state predictor with a "transition-aware mixture of weather experts" (MeteoMoE) module injected residually into a pretrained video-diffusion (DiT) backbone.
**Purpose (≤3 sentences):** The authors argue real-world scene evolution depends on environmental/weather conditions as well as viewpoint and object dynamics, and that prior video world models entangle weather inference with scene dynamics, making controllable weather generation unreliable. MeteoVerse aims to disentangle weather from scene modeling so that the same target weather condition (e.g. "rainy") is handled correctly whether it must be introduced, preserved, or removed.
**Breakthrough (≤3 sentences):** The authors report large gains over baselines on their own weather-controllable video benchmark: weather introduction alignment of 61.00 versus 29.00 for the best baseline (VerseCrafter), and weather removal alignment of 89.00 versus 38.00 for LingBot-World, while weather-preservation scores remain competitive (86.70 vs. 86.56). They also report the weather-state predictor achieves 89.75% state accuracy under a 0.10 component-wise tolerance.
**Tools & method (≤3 sentences):** The pipeline includes a new MeteoVerse dataset of over 50,000 real-world weather video clips with pseudo-paired "sunny" counterparts, disentangled scene/weather descriptions, intensity annotations, and camera trajectories, supporting four training-pair types (preservation/introduction/removal). Training used 8 NVIDIA A800-SXM4 GPUs in BF16 with progressive resolution/frame scaling (21→41→81 frames, 240×416→480×832) and an AdamW optimizer with cosine decay.
**Limitation (≤3 sentences):** The paper notes that imperfect weather-state prediction has some downstream impact, with weather alignment dropping from 82 to 81 and minor degradation in VLM/user-study scores when predicted (versus ground-truth) weather states are used. Camera-control precision (~3.4° rotation error, ~0.13 translation error) is reported as merely "competitive" rather than best-in-class.

## LEGO-Anything: Coding Agents for 3D Scene Reconstruction
- **arXiv:** 2609.36380 · https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36380
- **Submitted:** 2026-09-28
- **Authors:** Xirui Li, Peng Shi, Mingwen Dong, Sheng Zhang, Zhuoyan Xu, Dongkyu Lee, Shuaichen Chang, Yi Xiang, Lin Pan, Jiarong Jiang
- **Qualifying affiliation(s):** AWS (Amazon) — Peng Shi, Mingwen Dong, Sheng Zhang, Zhuoyan Xu, Dongkyu Lee, Shuaichen Chang, Yi Xiang, Lin Pan, Jiarong Jiang; Xirui Li is at University of Maryland, College Park, with the work done during an AWS internship
- **Categories:** cs.CV
- **Open release:** none (project page only: https://lego-anything.com; no explicit code/weights release mentioned in the fetched text, though the Harbor evaluation framework is referenced)
- **Shipped counterpart:** none found

**Summary (≤3 sentences):** LEGO-Anything reframes single-image 3D scene reconstruction as an "Image-to-Code" problem, where general-purpose coding agents iteratively write, execute, and refine Blender programs that render and are compared against the input image, producing an editable, queryable 3D scene as executable code rather than a fixed 3D output. The authors also introduce LEGO-Bench, a simulator-grounded 208-image benchmark scoring artifact validity, geometry, and appearance.
**Purpose (≤3 sentences):** The goal is to move beyond fixed-format 3D reconstruction outputs toward an inspectable, editable scene representation (Blender code) built through iterative agentic refinement guided by visual comparison to the source image.
**Breakthrough (≤3 sentences):** The authors report their best-performing coding agent, GPT-6-astra, scores 53.4% indoor and 39.6% outdoor on LEGO-Bench, and identify three recurring failure modes: weak scene initialization, regressive edits during iteration, and unreliable self-evaluation. A training-free "LEGO-Plugin" addressing these failure modes improves scores by up to 62.7% relative gain for some models (55-63% for GPT-5.6 models, 27.5% for GPT-6-luna, 2.1% for GPT-6-astra).
**Tools & method (≤3 sentences):** The benchmark uses the LychSim simulator with Fab assets (443 registered assets across 8 environments), evaluated through the Harbor Framework with Blender 5.0.1 and Blender-MCP, testing GPT-6 (astra/sol/luna) and GPT-5.6 model families via a Codex-style coding-agent harness. A "LEGO-World" evaluation shows the reconstructed scenes can support detection, segmentation, and depth as deterministic readouts, though these underperform specialized vision models (e.g., detection AP 30.14 vs. DINO's 59.88; depth AbsRel 0.1554 vs. Depth Anything 3's 0.0783).
**Limitation (≤3 sentences):** The authors state that weaker agents take longer to produce an evaluable scene and subsequent edits can degrade fidelity, that agents "cannot reliably judge scene quality, especially geometry" (self-judging offers no advantage), and that outdoor scenes are harder than indoor scenes at every capability tier. They also note reconstructed scenes "fall well short of specialized vision models" for downstream perception tasks.

## Providing Rapid Design Feedback for 3D Obstacle Course Games Using Constrained Solvability Queries
- **arXiv:** 2609.36225 · https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36225
- **Submitted:** 2026-09-28
- **Authors:** Zander Majercik, Sharon Zhang, William Wang, Tejan Karmali, Fangjun Zhou, Yucheng Yuan, Jean-Peïc Chou, Maneesh Agrawala, Kayvon Fatahalian
- **Qualifying affiliation(s):** Roblox — Zander Majercik, Sharon Zhang, Maneesh Agrawala, Kayvon Fatahalian (dual-affiliated with Stanford University); other authors solely at Stanford University
- **Categories:** cs.GR
- **Open release:** code — https://zandermajercik.github.io/interactive-obstacle-course-feedback/ (paper states "we release code for our interactive tool, simulator, training setup, and procedural level generation system")
- **Shipped counterpart:** none found

**Summary (≤3 sentences):** The paper presents an interactive design tool that gives 3D obstacle-course game designers rapid feedback by answering "constrained solvability queries" (e.g., can a waypoint be reached while avoiding a region, within a jump/time budget) using a GPU-accelerated digital-twin simulator built on the Madrona engine. It combines a modified Go-Explore search algorithm with a pretrained reinforcement-learning policy ("Go-Explore with Policy Selection") to find qualifying player action sequences within seconds.
**Purpose (≤3 sentences):** Manual playtesting or hiring external testers to evaluate obstacle-course navigability is slow and disruptive to creative iteration, so the authors aim to give designers near-instant, constraint-based feedback on how players can traverse and potentially break a level design.
**Breakthrough (≤3 sentences):** The authors report the custom GPU-accelerated simulator runs about 14,000× real-time gameplay (over 800K game steps/second across 8K parallel simulations), solving most queries within seconds, and that Policy Selection reduces average solve time by more than 15 seconds on long queries versus baseline Go-Explore variants. Design studies with four external designers and playtesting with six participants showed the tool surfaced unintended solution paths and let players reproduce designer-validated intended paths.
**Tools & method (≤3 sentences):** The system is built in Roblox Studio, using a discretized state space (binned by position, elapsed time, jump count, object positions) for Go-Explore search, a reinforcement-learning policy pretrained on procedurally generated obstacle courses, and an RTX 4090 GPU for inference; queries and found trajectories stream to the client for real-time visualization. Evaluation used 56 solvability queries logged during design studies plus human playtesting with six college-age participants.
**Limitation (≤3 sentences):** The authors state that policies pretrained only on procedural data "failed to reliably generalize to novel human-designed obstacles," requiring the hybrid policy+random-search approach, and that fine-tuning policies within interactive latency budgets was impractical. They also note that building accurate navigation meshes for complex 3D obstacles with moving parts "is an overly complex problem that is not viable," and that the approach is specialized to obstacle-course games and may not transfer to other genres.
