## FL4N: Feed-forward Latent Model for 4D Novel View Synthesis
- **arXiv:** 2610.35854 · https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35854
- **Submitted:** 2026-10-26
- **Authors:** Eunbeen Hong, Hyeonseo Yu, Honggyu An, Jooho Son, Hyunah Ko, Soowon Son, Chaehyun Kim, Jahyeok Koo, Jisoo Kim, Junhwa Hur, Seungryong Kim
- **Qualifying affiliation(s):** Google DeepMind — the HTML author block lists two team affiliations, "KAIST AI" and "Google DeepMind," for the full author group; the page carries no per-author superscripts, so the exact author(s) tied to Google DeepMind cannot be isolated from the page itself.
- **Categories:** cs.CV
- **Open release:** code + weights — GitHub: github.com/cvlab-kaist/FL4N (link present on project page; direct fetch returned HTTP 403, likely bot-blocking rather than absence); HuggingFace model page: huggingface.co/eunbeen-hong/FL4N (returned HTTP 200); project page: https://cvlab-kaist.github.io/FL4N/
- **Shipped counterpart:** none found

**Summary (≤3 sentences):** FL4N is presented as the first feed-forward latent model for 4D novel view synthesis (NVS), rendering dynamic scenes across new viewpoints and timestamps from monocular video without explicit 4D Gaussian primitives. It encodes a 4D scene prior into a single space-time latent and learns dynamics as residuals over a pretrained static latent prior. A lightweight dynamic encoder disentangles object motion from camera-induced parallax using static scene features.
**Purpose (≤3 sentences):** The paper targets a limitation of existing monocular feed-forward 4D NVS methods, which rely on explicit 4D Gaussian representations and are prone to temporal inconsistency and rendering artifacts. It aims to produce a unified latent representation of dynamic scenes that avoids these per-primitive artifacts.
**Breakthrough (≤3 sentences):** The authors report that modeling temporal dynamics as residuals on top of a pretrained static latent prior enables effective 4D modeling even with limited dynamic training data. Trained solely with an image synthesis objective, the authors report FL4N achieves state-of-the-art 4D NVS quality while rendering in real time, and that its latent representation generalizes to real-world videos and disentangles viewpoint from time.
**Tools & method (≤3 sentences):** The method is a feed-forward latent 4D NVS model built on a static latent prior plus a residual dynamic encoder trained with an image synthesis objective (no explicit 4D Gaussian splatting). The paper does not state specific benchmark numbers in the abstract beyond "state-of-the-art quality" and "real-time" rendering.
**Limitation (≤3 sentences):** The paper's own stated limitation (Appendix G) is that the residual formulation can compensate for certain errors in the static prediction, but its capacity is naturally limited when the underlying scene representation or camera pose is substantially incorrect. The authors note this could be alleviated with stronger static NVS backbones with broader scene coverage and more robust viewpoint understanding.

## Generative Structure from Motion with Native 3D Diffusion
- **arXiv:** 2610.35522 · https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35522
- **Submitted:** 2026-10-26
- **Authors:** Haobin Duan, Binbin Huang, Mochu Xiang, Yiqun Zhao, Zibo Zhao, Yi Ma, Shenghua Gao
- **Qualifying affiliation(s):** Tencent — Zibo Zhao (the HTML page's per-author affiliation block tags Zibo Zhao solely with "Tencent"; the other six authors are tagged "The University of Hong Kong" and "Transengram").
- **Categories:** cs.CV, cs.AI
- **Open release:** none found beyond a project page (https://dotb1ue.github.io/GenSfm/); no code or weights link was located on that page or in the paper text.
- **Shipped counterpart:** none found

**Summary (≤3 sentences):** The paper proposes GenSfM, which recovers 3D object structure and camera pose from a few uncalibrated views by integrating image-based 3D reconstruction with a native 3D diffusion model. A multi-modal diffusion transformer jointly denoises a shape latent and per-view pose latents under shared self-attention, grounding generated geometry in observed views while using object-level priors to infer unobserved regions. A compact 3D-native UV-volume tokenization keeps the joint sequence small as views are added, followed by a pose-aware refinement stage.
**Purpose (≤3 sentences):** The authors target the gap between classical structure-from-motion methods (geometrically faithful but unable to recover unseen regions) and native 3D generation methods (plausible but not necessarily faithful to input images), and the error cascading that occurs when the two are simply concatenated.
**Breakthrough (≤3 sentences):** On Toys4k and GSO with 4 uncalibrated views, the authors report GenSfM improves novel-view PSNR by 3.2–4.5 dB and reduces median Chamfer distance by 2.8–3.8x over a strong native-3D generation baseline. They also report recovering input-view poses with over 96% Acc@5° without any external pose-estimation backbone, post-hoc optimization, or known intrinsics.
**Tools & method (≤3 sentences):** The method uses a multi-modal diffusion transformer with shared self-attention to jointly denoise shape and pose latents, a 3D-native UV-volume tokenization, and a pose-aware refinement stage that samples per-voxel image features at recovered camera poses. It builds on a TRELLIS-based generative backbone (per the paper's stated limitations) and is evaluated on Toys4k and Google Scanned Objects (GSO).
**Limitation (≤3 sentences):** The paper states its results are restricted to object-centric inputs at N=4 views, with scene-level inputs and in-the-wild captures left as future work. It also states that under distribution shift, large pose/calibration errors may misregister fine-stage image features, and that the model inherits the object distribution and resolution limits of its TRELLIS backbone.

## DriveUniTok: A Unified Multimodal Tokenizer for Driving World-Action Models
- **arXiv:** 2610.35044 · https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35044
- **Submitted:** 2026-10-26
- **Authors:** Jingbo Xu, Hanchao Leng, JiaHui Huang, Hanbing Li, Jingwei Zhao, Kuiyuan Yang, Chen Gao, Guang Chen, Hangjun Ye, Yong Li
- **Qualifying affiliation(s):** Xiaomi EV — Hanchao Leng (project leader), JiaHui Huang, Hanbing Li, Jingwei Zhao, Kuiyuan Yang, Guang Chen, Hangjun Ye, and Jingbo Xu (dual-affiliated with Tsinghua University). - **Categories:** cs.CV, cs.RO
- **Open release:** none found — no project page, code, or weights link located in the paper.
- **Shipped counterpart:** none found

**Summary (≤3 sentences):** DriveUniTok is a unified multimodal tokenizer for driving world-action models (WAMs) that encodes appearance, projected-LiDAR metric geometry, and ego motion into a single decodable continuous latent, rather than modeling them separately. A frozen DriveUniTok is paired with DriveUniWAM, a pretrained diffusion model adapted to predict future unified latents, which the tokenizer then decodes back into future images, projected LiDAR depth, and ego trajectories.
**Purpose (≤3 sentences):** The paper argues that existing driving world-model approaches model appearance, LiDAR-projected depth, and ego motion partially or independently, despite these being different projections of one underlying world state, causing information loss and cross-modal inconsistency that limits downstream planning performance.
**Breakthrough (≤3 sentences):** On the NAVSIM benchmark, the authors report that the unified tokenizer design achieves better future prediction and planning performance at lower downstream computational cost than separate-stream alternatives. With reinforcement-learning post-training, the authors report DriveUniWAM reaches a PDMS of 91.54, which they state is state-of-the-art among the WAM-based planners they evaluated.
**Tools & method (≤3 sentences):** The method freezes the DriveUniTok tokenizer and adapts a pretrained diffusion model (DriveUniWAM) to predict future unified latents, which are decoded into future appearance, projected LiDAR depth, and ego trajectories; evaluation is on the NAVSIM benchmark, with an added reinforcement-learning post-training stage.
**Limitation (≤3 sentences):** The paper does not contain an explicit "Limitations" section in the HTML version. (observed, not stated) The approach depends on projected LiDAR depth as its geometry signal and is evaluated only in the NAVSIM simulation benchmark, so real-world/on-road generalization is not directly demonstrated in the abstract or available text.

## PosePlaner: Denoising Any Feed-Forward Pose Predictor from Pairwise Planar Geometry
- **arXiv:** 2610.34845 · https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34845
- **Submitted:** 2026-10-26
- **Authors:** Yihang Chen, Bin Tan, Yujun Shen, Yinghao Xu, Nan Xue
- **Qualifying affiliation(s):** Ant Group (Robbyant) — Bin Tan (superscript 2, corresponding author), Yujun Shen (superscript 2), Nan Xue (superscript 2); Yihang Chen is dual-affiliated (HKUST + Ant Group, superscript 1,2). FLAG: borderline (Ant Group is explicitly named as a borderline example in the task brief).
- **Categories:** cs.CV
- **Open release:** none found beyond a project page (https://robbyant-research.github.io/PosePlaner/); no code or weights link was located. Comments on the abstract page note the paper was accepted at NeurIPS 2026.
- **Shipped counterpart:** none found

**Summary (≤3 sentences):** PosePlaner refines camera poses from any feed-forward multi-view 3D predictor (e.g., DUSt3R, VGGT) using flow matching conditioned on pairwise planar geometry, treating pose refinement as a conditional generation problem. It takes coarse geometric matches and an initial pose as input and fuses resulting pose hypotheses into one refined estimate, acting as a continuous, learned analog of RANSAC voting.
**Purpose (≤3 sentences):** The paper addresses the fact that modern feed-forward multi-view Transformers collapse the classical SfM/SLAM pipeline (feature matching, geometry, pose) into a single forward pass, which hides the interfaces between stages and leaves supervised fine-tuning as the main path to improving them, with no mechanism to correct predictions from geometric evidence at test time.
**Breakthrough (≤3 sentences):** The authors report that PosePlaner, trained only on two-view data, can periodically refine streaming n-view pose estimators without retraining. They report that it corrects feed-forward predictor failures, substantially improves correspondence-based solvers, and reduces accumulated drift in long streaming sequences; on ScanNet1500 two-view refinement experiments are reported in Table 1 of the paper.
**Tools & method (≤3 sentences):** The method uses flow matching as a single-Euler-step diffusion adapter conditioned on matched planar primitives for rotation refinement, combined with geometric translation recovery, and extends to n-view streaming via per-edge view-graph refinement aggregated by rotation averaging and a Glomap-style positioner. It is evaluated against correspondence-based (e.g., ROMA, MASt3R) and diffusion-based feed-forward pose estimators, including on ScanNet1500.
**Limitation (≤3 sentences):** The paper states that PosePlaner relies on reliable planar evidence, so scenes with limited planar structure or inaccurate plane predictions can reduce refinement gains. It also states that for already-accurate initial poses, plane-prediction noise can outweigh the remaining correction opportunity and cause slight regressions, as observed for ROMA and MASt3R on ScanNet1500.

## TurboWAM: Learning What Matters for Fast World Action Models
- **arXiv:** 2610.33941 · https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33941
- **Submitted:** 2026-10-24
- **Authors:** Guile Wu, Shuangzhi Li, Xingxin Chen, Chengjie Huang, Sam Hosseini, Tongtong Cao, Yingxue Zhang, Dongfeng Bai
- **Qualifying affiliation(s):** Huawei (Noah's Ark Lab / Department of Foundation Model) — Guile Wu, Xingxin Chen, Chengjie Huang, Sam Hosseini, Tongtong Cao, Yingxue Zhang, Dongfeng Bai, and Shuangzhi Li (dual-affiliated with University of Alberta; footnote states the work was done during an internship at Huawei Canada). FLAG: borderline (Huawei is explicitly named as a borderline example in the task brief).
- **Categories:** cs.CV
- **Open release:** none found beyond a project page (https://turbowam.github.io/); the only GitHub link on that page is a template credit (nerfies), not the authors' own code.
- **Shipped counterpart:** none found

**Summary (≤3 sentences):** TurboWAM is a fast world action model (WAM) for robot policy learning that reduces inference latency by adaptively aggregating world-model-layer representations according to their relevance to action prediction, rather than using every layer uniformly. It introduces a structured world-representation aggregation module and a structured action-supervision mechanism that modulates gradient flow across layers during training.
**Purpose (≤3 sentences):** The paper targets high inference latency in world action models, a bottleneck for real-world deployment, noting that prior efficiency methods reduce future-frame imagination or speed up action prediction but still use every world-model layer without weighting their relevance to action prediction, causing redundant computation.
**Breakthrough (≤3 sentences):** At inference, the authors report TurboWAM runs fewer world-model layers and caches representations across denoising steps to substantially reduce computational overhead. They report that on multiple robot manipulation benchmarks TurboWAM achieves low inference latency while maintaining task success rates competitive with state-of-the-art methods (the abstract does not give specific latency/success-rate numbers).
**Tools & method (≤3 sentences):** The method combines a structured world-representation aggregation module (adaptively fusing different world-model layers based on relevance to the current action-denoising state) with a structured action-supervision mechanism (modulating gradients across layers during backpropagation), plus layer reduction and representation caching at inference. It is evaluated on multiple robot manipulation benchmarks, including comparisons noted against a corrected-prompt variant of LIBERO (LIBERO-plus).
**Limitation (≤3 sentences):** The paper states that TurboWAM may face challenges in scenarios requiring complex spatial reasoning and intricate object interactions, suggesting geometric priors from spatial foundation models or semantic knowledge from vision-language models as possible remedies. It also states that further improving task success rates while preserving low inference latency remains an open research direction.

## WorldCast: World-Consistent Character Video Generation with Large-Angle Camera Control
- **arXiv:** 2610.33135 · https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33135
- **Submitted:** 2026-10-23
- **Authors:** Lei Cao, Yu Liu, Wenhua Liao, Peng Zhang, Fei Wang, Hao Zhang, Jiajun Le, Daiguo Zhou, Jian Luan, Jiayi Ma
- **Qualifying affiliation(s):** Xiaomi Inc. (MiLM Plus) — the HTML affiliation block lists "MiLM Plus, Xiaomi Inc." as affiliation 2 alongside Wuhan University's Electronic Information School (1) and School of Robotics (3); the page does not attach superscripts to individual author names, so the specific Xiaomi-affiliated author(s) cannot be isolated from the HTML. FLAG: borderline (Xiaomi is not on the default illustrative company list but is a large, top-tier consumer tech/AI company; flagged per the brief's instruction not to silently drop borderline cases).
- **Categories:** cs.CV
- **Open release:** code + weights + dataset — GitHub: https://github.com/PixCtrol/WorldCast; dataset (CLACT) on HuggingFace: https://huggingface.co/datasets/ly56/CLACT; model card on HuggingFace: https://huggingface.co/ly56/worldcast; project page: https://pixctrol.github.io/WorldCast/
- **Shipped counterpart:** none found

**Summary (≤3 sentences):** WorldCast is a framework for world-consistent character video generation under large-angle camera trajectories that combine rotation, translation, and radius change from arbitrary starting azimuths. It back-projects the first frame into a 3D point cloud using estimated depth, scales that point cloud to the target camera trajectory, and renders it along the trajectory to produce geometric guidance and coverage masks, then fills in missing character regions with a new attention mechanism.
**Purpose (≤3 sentences):** The paper addresses the problem that existing camera-controlled video generation methods struggle with large-angle viewpoint changes that reveal character regions not visible in the first frame, causing geometric distortion, appearance degradation, and identity drift. To study this systematically, the authors also introduce CLACT, a large-scale character-centric dataset and benchmark with accurate camera geometry and multi-view character observations.
**Breakthrough (≤3 sentences):** The authors introduce Geometry-Aligned Memory Attention (GAMA), which uses relative camera poses estimated jointly from the initial frame and multi-view character anchors to express anchor and target-view geometry in a common coordinate frame, then retrieves and injects view-relevant character features during generation. The authors report accurate trajectory following while preserving character geometry, appearance, and identity across diverse large-angle camera trajectories (the abstract does not give specific quantitative metrics).
**Tools & method (≤3 sentences):** The pipeline combines depth-based point-cloud back-projection and re-rendering along the target trajectory (for geometric guidance and coverage masks) with GAMA, a memory-attention mechanism that aligns and retrieves multi-view character-anchor features. It is trained and evaluated on the authors' own CLACT dataset/benchmark, with code, the CLACT dataset, and a model card released on GitHub/HuggingFace.
**Limitation (≤3 sentences):** The paper's conclusion does not state explicit limitations in the available text. (observed, not stated) The method depends on estimated monocular depth for its point-cloud guidance step, so its accuracy under depth-estimation failure or very sparse initial-frame character coverage is not addressed in the abstract or conclusion.
